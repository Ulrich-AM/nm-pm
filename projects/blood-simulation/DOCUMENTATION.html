Real-Time Hybrid Blood Simulation for 2.5D Environments

A living technical study of how ballistic droplets, persistent surface deposits, accumulation, flow, dripping, puddling, smearing, transfer, and drying can be approximated efficiently in an orthographic 2.5D side-room environment backed by a mathematically three-dimensional world.

Phase 4 keeps most deposited blood stationary. Only selected wet local peaks become active drip channels, with spacing limits and regional cooldowns. Horizontal accumulation spreads with an isotropic eight-neighbor stencil, and unsupported-edge detachment is rate-limited.

A short support probe catches partial-contact cases such as box sides sitting directly above the floor, preventing the below-floor particle stream observed in Phase 3.

Research Problem

How can blood behavior in a 2.5D orthographic side-room environment remain spatially coherent as it moves between ballistic world-space motion and persistent surface-space state, while remaining cheap enough for large quantities of blood?

A key early subproblem is persistent surface representation. Premade raster decals, procedural vector splats, procedurally generated raster deposits, persistent raster fields, and hybrid approaches remain competing hypotheses rather than requirements.

World Model

The visual presentation is a 2.5D orthographic side-room / beat-em-up view, but simulation space is three-dimensional:

X = room width
Y = room depth
Z = height

Player locomotion currently uses the XY floor plane only. X is left/right, Y is front/back depth, and Z is height. The camera has no perspective distance scaling, so movement toward the back wall changes screen position but not object size. Surface impacts and future droplets use full XYZ coordinates. No persistent simulation state is stored in screen space.

Surface Contract

Phase 0 uses finite planar rectangular surfaces. Each surface owns a world origin, two tangent axes, dimensions, a derived normal, and a stable identifier.

P = O + uU + vV

This gives every floor, wall, and box face its own persistent two-dimensional coordinate system while keeping an exact relationship to the three-dimensional room.

Gravity on a surface

The shared mathematical layer includes the future flow primitive:

g_surface = g - dot(g, n) n

On a horizontal floor this approaches zero. On a vertical wall it remains downward. Phase 0 tests this calculation but does not yet move blood with it.

Impact Contract

A successful surface hit becomes a structured event containing the surface identifier and kind, world XYZ position, surface-local U/V, normal, incoming direction, incident angle, speed, volume, droplet radius, and deterministic seed.

Phase 1 representation candidates will receive the same event structure so their outputs can be compared under identical inputs.

Phase 1 Representation Lab

The Phase 1 native executable is phases/phase1/main.cpp. Clicking a visible surface places the current controlled impact event at that surface-local position. Switching representation changes how the same stored event is rendered rather than moving or regenerating the hit location.

Impact speed, incident angle, volume, local direction, deterministic seed, camera yaw, and camera pitch are adjustable from the left panel.

Five Representation Candidates

  • Premade raster: four fixed shape signatures form the cheap repetition baseline.
  • Procedural vector: central body, lobes, streaks, and satellites remain live geometry.
  • Generated raster: procedural geometry is constructed once and rasterized into a persistent local patch.
  • Surface field: blood is deposited directly into a local field representation intended to support later material state.
  • Hybrid: a coarse raster carries the central deposit while thin streaks and satellites remain procedural.

Measurements

Every representation is converted to a common evaluation raster without forcing the runtime representation itself to be raster-based. The lab reports generation time, estimated memory, major-axis direction error, primitive/cell count, 16-seed diversity, and qualitative merge potential.

E1 Representation Sweep

The Phase 1 lab can evaluate 24 deterministic impact events across all five methods and export:

results/phase1/E1_representation_metrics.csv
results/phase1/E1_representation_summary.csv

No method is promoted solely because it is fastest. Numeric evidence must be combined with visual review and suitability for Phase 2 accumulation, wetness, aging, and freezing.

Phase 2 Persistent Field Lab

The current native harness is phases/phase2/main.cpp. Impact properties, lifecycle controls, camera controls, and test actions live in the left sidebar. The main room occupies the central panel. Live field metrics, E2 export, and the event log occupy the bottom strip.

This layout intentionally matches the completed Sprite Projection methodology rather than the Phase 1 three-column dashboard.

Blood Morphology

The Phase 2 generator constructs a persistent stain from a dense irregular central ellipse, perimeter lobes, thin directional streaks, satellite droplets, and higher-energy mist. Speed, incidence, direction, and volume control the high-level form while a deterministic seed controls small-scale irregularity.

The target is a recognizable deposited blood pattern. Glossy free-flight splash sprites are explicitly outside this surface-stain model.

Static Surface Wrapping

Each planar surface exposes four finite edges. Exact shared world-space edge segments are linked automatically. If a deposit sample crosses a linked edge, its overflow distance is transformed into the neighboring surface's local coordinates. This allows crate-top stains to continue onto crate sides and floor stains to continue onto the back wall.

If no neighbor exists, the sample is clipped instead of becoming floating geometry.

This is static deposit continuation only. Gravity-driven cross-surface liquid flow remains a later experiment.

Sparse Persistent Field

Each surface allocates 16 × 16 field tiles only where blood exists. Every cell stores amount, wetness, age, local velocity channels, and a frozen flag. Repeated impacts accumulate amount and refresh wetness. Dry cells eventually freeze and stop lifecycle updates while remaining visible.

Blood cells are rendered as world-space quads inside the normal 3D depth pass, so ordinary scene occlusion applies.

Phase 3 Gravity Flow Lab

The current native harness is phases/phase3/main.cpp. It preserves the Phase 2 sparse field and adds local surface flow plus world-space droplets. Flow runs in fixed substeps of at most 1/120 s, even when the visible time scale is increased.

Blended Field Rendering

The simulation field remains discrete, but rendering samples a weighted 3 × 3 neighborhood. Each surface cell is rendered once from the merged field amount. Interior blood is close to opaque and only the feathered perimeter uses lower alpha.

This replaces the earlier appearance of translucent images stacked on top of each other without blurring the underlying simulation state every frame.

Repeated-Impact Variation

The seed control is now a base seed. With repeated-impact variation enabled, each hit derives a deterministic event seed from the base seed plus impact serial, then applies small bounded perturbations to direction, speed, incidence, and volume.

Turning variation off restores exact repeatability for controlled comparisons.

Gravity-Driven Surface Flow

World gravity is projected onto each supporting surface and converted into its local U/V basis. Wet blood above an adhesion threshold transfers toward neighboring cells. On upward horizontal surfaces, local amount differences drive a slower four-neighbor pressure spread that forms puddles.

At a supported wall base, downward flow may transfer directly into an upward-facing neighboring surface. General arbitrary connected-surface flow remains reserved for Phase 4.

Detached World Droplets

When enough wet blood reaches an unsupported edge, material is removed from the field and converted to a world-space droplet. The droplet accelerates under XYZ gravity, sweeps its motion segment against the room surfaces, and generates a new surface-local deposit on impact.

The Phase 3 lab exports:

The deterministic 45-second sweep records wet and frozen cells, active droplets, puddle cells, total amount, moved amount, cumulative detachments, and cumulative landings.

Phase 4 Sparse Channel Lab

The current native harness is phases/phase4/main.cpp. It preserves the Phase 3 blended field and world droplets but replaces field-wide wall advection with a small set of active drip sources.

Sparse Drip Regions

A wet wall cell may become a drip source only if it has enough amount and wetness, forms a local peak across the gravity direction, passes deterministic spatial selection, is separated from other sources, and is outside a regional cooldown.

Each source advances a small packet one cell at a time. The root must overcome adhesion, while an already-formed packet may continue farther and leave a decaying residue trail. This produces a few narrow lines rather than moving the entire stain.

Rounded Puddle Spreading

Upward-facing surfaces now use an eight-neighbor pressure-spread stencil. Cardinal and diagonal neighbors are weighted so floor deposits expand more isotropically and approach rounded footprints rather than the older diamond-like pattern.

Support Probing and Detachment

Exact connected topology is attempted first when a drip reaches an edge. If no exact edge match exists, a short downward probe searches for an upward-facing supporting surface. A detected floor receives the blood directly.

Only genuinely unsupported channels may create a world droplet. Detachment also requires enough packet mass, a clear global cooldown, and room under the active-droplet cap. Swept collision accepts near-zero floor hits so droplets cannot skip a valid floor simply because they began close to it.

Contact Transfer and Smearing

The player now carries a small temporary blood reservoir. Crossing wet floor blood removes only a bounded pickup amount. After enough movement distance, some carried blood is deposited as a short weighted smear aligned to the dominant movement direction.

Pickup and smearing use the same persistent field as impact splats, puddles, and dried blood rather than a separate footprint decal system.

Irregular Surface Direction

Arbitrary geometry is deferred, but Phase 0 avoids assumptions that would prevent it later. The intended extension is a connected patch or triangle model. Each patch exposes a normal, local coordinates, and neighboring edges. Flow crossing a shared edge transfers to its neighbor, while an open edge may accumulate blood or detach a world-space droplet.

Static irregular surfaces must be validated before moving or deforming surfaces are attempted.

Implementation Roadmap

  1. Phase 0: true-XYZ room and reusable surface foundation;
  2. Phase 1: static splat generation and representation comparison;
  3. Phase 2: persistent field, accumulation, wetness, age, drying, and freezing;
  4. Phase 3: gravity flow, walls, drips, puddles, and detached droplets;
  5. Phase 4: connected surfaces, corner transfer, smearing, and contact transfer;
  6. Phase 5: arbitrary polygonal and irregular static surfaces;
  7. Phase 6: scaling tests and final hybrid reference architecture.

Phase 4 reuses the persistent field and world-droplet representation but replaces broad wall flow with sparse deterministic channel sources, partial support probing, eight-neighbor puddles, and a contact-transfer reservoir.

Current Limitations

  • drip sources are discrete local channels rather than continuous fluid filaments;
  • puddles use pressure equalization rather than a free-surface height solver;
  • partial support probing handles nearby upward surfaces but is not arbitrary mesh adjacency;
  • contact transfer currently samples the main upward-facing floor only;
  • surface roughness, porosity, and absorption are not yet material parameters;
  • droplets remain simple spheres without aerodynamic breakup;
  • irregular and deforming geometry remain future work.
