Real-Time Hybrid Blood Simulation for 2.5D Environments

A living technical study of how ballistic droplets, persistent surface deposits, accumulation, flow, dripping, puddling, smearing, transfer, and drying can be approximated efficiently in an orthographic 2.5D side-room environment backed by a mathematically three-dimensional world.

Research Problem

How can blood behavior in a 2.5D orthographic side-room environment remain spatially coherent as it moves between ballistic world-space motion and persistent surface-space state, while remaining cheap enough for large quantities of blood?

A key early subproblem is persistent surface representation. Premade raster decals, procedural vector splats, procedurally generated raster deposits, persistent raster fields, and hybrid approaches remain competing hypotheses rather than requirements.

World Model

The visual presentation is a 2.5D orthographic side-room / beat-em-up view, but simulation space is three-dimensional:

X = room width
Y = room depth
Z = height

Player locomotion currently uses the XY floor plane only. X is left/right, Y is front/back depth, and Z is height. The camera has no perspective distance scaling, so movement toward the back wall changes screen position but not object size. Surface impacts and future droplets use full XYZ coordinates. No persistent simulation state is stored in screen space.

Surface Contract

Phase 0 uses finite planar rectangular surfaces. Each surface owns a world origin, two tangent axes, dimensions, a derived normal, and a stable identifier.

P = O + uU + vV

This gives every floor, wall, and box face its own persistent two-dimensional coordinate system while keeping an exact relationship to the three-dimensional room.

Gravity on a surface

The shared mathematical layer includes the future flow primitive:

g_surface = g - dot(g, n) n

On a horizontal floor this approaches zero. On a vertical wall it remains downward. Phase 0 tests this calculation but does not yet move blood with it.

Impact Contract

A successful surface hit becomes a structured event containing the surface identifier and kind, world XYZ position, surface-local U/V, normal, incoming direction, incident angle, speed, volume, droplet radius, and deterministic seed.

Phase 1 representation candidates will receive the same event structure so their outputs can be compared under identical inputs.

Phase 1 Representation Lab

The Phase 1 native executable is phases/phase1/main.cpp. Clicking a visible surface places the current controlled impact event at that surface-local position. Switching representation changes how the same stored event is rendered rather than moving or regenerating the hit location.

Impact speed, incident angle, volume, local direction, deterministic seed, camera yaw, and camera pitch are adjustable from the left panel.

Five Representation Candidates

  • Premade raster: four fixed shape signatures form the cheap repetition baseline.
  • Procedural vector: central body, lobes, streaks, and satellites remain live geometry.
  • Generated raster: procedural geometry is constructed once and rasterized into a persistent local patch.
  • Surface field: blood is deposited directly into a local field representation intended to support later material state.
  • Hybrid: a coarse raster carries the central deposit while thin streaks and satellites remain procedural.

Measurements

Every representation is converted to a common evaluation raster without forcing the runtime representation itself to be raster-based. The lab reports generation time, estimated memory, major-axis direction error, primitive/cell count, 16-seed diversity, and qualitative merge potential.

E1 Representation Sweep

The Phase 1 lab can evaluate 24 deterministic impact events across all five methods and export:

results/phase1/E1_representation_metrics.csv
results/phase1/E1_representation_summary.csv

No method is promoted solely because it is fastest. Numeric evidence must be combined with visual review and suitability for Phase 2 accumulation, wetness, aging, and freezing.

Irregular Surface Direction

Arbitrary geometry is deferred, but Phase 0 avoids assumptions that would prevent it later. The intended extension is a connected patch or triangle model. Each patch exposes a normal, local coordinates, and neighboring edges. Flow crossing a shared edge transfers to its neighbor, while an open edge may accumulate blood or detach a world-space droplet.

Static irregular surfaces must be validated before moving or deforming surfaces are attempted.

Implementation Roadmap

  1. Phase 0: true-XYZ room and reusable surface foundation;
  2. Phase 1: static splat generation and representation comparison;
  3. Phase 2: persistent field, accumulation, wetness, age, drying, and freezing;
  4. Phase 3: gravity flow, walls, drips, puddles, and detached droplets;
  5. Phase 4: connected surfaces, corner transfer, smearing, and contact transfer;
  6. Phase 5: arbitrary polygonal and irregular static surfaces;
  7. Phase 6: scaling tests and final hybrid reference architecture.

Current Limitations

  • Phase 1 blood marks are static;
  • field channels are structural candidates rather than time-updated material state;
  • only planar rectangular surfaces are implemented;
  • surface adjacency is still inactive;
  • there is no spreading, dripping, puddling, transfer, or drying yet;
  • moving and deforming surfaces remain outside the initial study scope.

AI Transparency

This study follows the repository-wide AI disclosure. Research direction, constraints, evaluation, visual review, and final decisions remain human-directed while implementation and technical exploration may use substantial generative-AI assistance.
