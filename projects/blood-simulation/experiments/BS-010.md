# BS-010 - Radial Floor Pooling and Isometric Camera Stress

**Phase:** 5  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `blood-simulation-v0.6.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-20

## Question

Can concentrated wet blood on upward-facing surfaces spread toward a rounded pool without introducing a full free-surface fluid solver, and does the behavior remain unchanged when the lab is viewed from an isometric angle?

## Motivation

Phase 4's eight-neighbor puddle pass reduced the earlier four-neighbor diamond shape, but floor accumulation could still reveal the grid and did not strongly communicate a circular pool around repeated landings.

## Method

Phase 5 uses a wider radial neighborhood on upward-facing surfaces.

For each eligible wet cell:

- a circular local neighborhood is sampled;
- pressure is estimated from source amount, neighbor amount, adhesion, and distance;
- Euclidean distance supplies a radial falloff;
- bounded deterministic variation perturbs the edge slightly;
- conservative scaling prevents requested transfers from exceeding source material.

Repeated landings, support transfers, and smears all feed the same field and can therefore merge into the same pool.

## Camera stress condition

The Phase 5 lab defaults to an orthographic isometric presentation at approximately 45 degrees yaw and 35.264 degrees pitch. Camera yaw and pitch remain adjustable.

No simulation state or transfer rule reads camera orientation.

## Success criteria

BS-010 is successful if:

- a concentrated floor deposit expands in both cardinal and diagonal directions;
- the occupied footprint has approximately similar width and height;
- total field amount is conserved within numerical tolerance during radial redistribution;
- landed world droplets become persistent floor material and subsequently participate in the radial pass;
- changing camera angle changes presentation only.


## v0.6.1 patch

Visual testing showed that the initial radial solver expanded too quickly. The patch lowers the default radial-pool control from 1.35 to 0.22 and reduces the per-step movable-material cap from 14% to 8%.

The default drying rate is also reduced from 0.038 to 0.008 so a new pool remains wet long enough for the slower motion to be visible.

The native lab additionally gains a surface-aware drag tool for controlled splash direction and force. This tool changes experiment input only and does not alter the camera-independence requirement.
