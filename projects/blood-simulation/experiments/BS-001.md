# BS-001 - World and Surface Coordinate Foundation

**Phase:** 0  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Date:** 2026-10-19

## Question

Can a 2.5D isometric room use a mathematically three-dimensional world and produce stable, representation-neutral surface impact data for floors, walls, and box-shaped obstacles?

## Motivation

Later blood experiments require persistent stains to remain attached to surfaces and eventually move according to surface orientation.

If the first prototype stores blood directly in screen space or assumes that every target is a horizontal plane, later wall drips, surface transfer, slopes, and irregular geometry would require a foundational rewrite.

BS-001 therefore tests the coordinate contract before selecting a blood representation.

## World model

World coordinates are:

```text
X = width
Y = room depth
Z = height
```

The test room contains:

- one horizontal floor;
- two vertical room walls;
- three box-shaped obstacles of different dimensions;
- a player constrained to the floor plane.

The isometric camera is orthographic and is treated only as a renderer.

## Surface model

Each planar surface stores:

- a world-space origin;
- local U and V tangent axes;
- a normal derived from those axes;
- finite U/V dimensions;
- stable identifier and surface kind.

The core transform is:

```text
P = O + u U + v V
```

World points can also be projected back into the local basis.

## Probe protocol

A screen click is converted into an orthographic world-space ray.

The world returns the nearest finite surface intersection.

The resulting Phase 0 impact event stores:

- hit surface;
- world XYZ position;
- local U/V position;
- normalized U/V position;
- surface normal;
- incoming direction;
- incident angle;
- speed;
- volume;
- droplet radius;
- deterministic seed.

The temporary red cross in the lab is only a debug marker. It is not the selected blood representation.

## Automated checks

The regression suite currently verifies:

1. local-to-world-to-local round trips;
2. floor ray intersection and local coordinates;
3. wall ray intersection and local coordinates;
4. nearest-hit selection when a box lies above the floor;
5. orthographic screen-ray consistency;
6. zero tangential gravity on a horizontal plane;
7. downward tangential gravity on a vertical wall.

## Success condition

Phase 0 is considered successful when later experiments can consume the impact event without needing camera-space assumptions or special-case floor/wall coordinate systems.

## Known limitations

- surfaces are planar rectangles;
- surface adjacency is reserved but not yet used;
- no slope or triangle-mesh flow is simulated;
- no blood amount, wetness, age, spreading, or drying exists yet;
- player movement is planar;
- collision is intentionally simple;
- the renderer uses whole-surface painter ordering and is not a general 3D renderer.

These limitations are intentional for BS-001.
