# BS-002 - Static Surface Blood Representation Comparison

**Phase:** 1  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `blood-simulation-v0.2.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-19

## Question

Which static surface-blood representation provides the most useful balance of visual variation, impact-direction fidelity, merge potential, memory use, and generation cost before dynamic flow is introduced?

## Camera update

Phase 1 keeps the orthographic side-room camera from BS-001 but increases the default yaw to approximately 11 degrees.

The camera remains orthographic. The additional yaw exists only to make side faces and obstacle depth easier to read.

The Phase 1 UI also exposes camera yaw and pitch as test properties so future view changes do not require changing the blood model.

## Compared representations

BS-002 implements five candidates:

1. **Premade raster baseline**
   - one of four fixed shape signatures is selected;
   - the mark is rotated and scaled for the current event;
   - intended to expose repetition and limited morphology.

2. **Procedural vector splat**
   - central deposit, lobes, streaks, and satellites are generated mathematically from the event;
   - geometry remains live for rendering.

3. **Procedurally generated raster splat**
   - the same procedural geometry is generated once;
   - the result is rasterized into a local patch and the vector geometry is discarded.

4. **Surface field candidate**
   - blood is deposited directly into a local raster field;
   - the field is evaluated as though later channels could contain amount, wetness, age, and velocity.

5. **Hybrid candidate**
   - a coarse generated raster stores the central deposit;
   - thin streaks and satellites remain procedural;
   - intended to test whether detailed outliers can remain cheap without requiring the entire stain to stay vector-based.

## Controlled impact parameters

The interactive Phase 1 lab exposes:

- impact speed;
- incident angle;
- blood volume;
- local surface direction;
- deterministic seed;
- camera yaw;
- camera pitch.

Clicking a visible surface places the same controlled event in that surface's local coordinate system.

Switching representation methods does not move the event.

## Measurements

For the selected representation the right-side panel reports:

- generation time in microseconds;
- estimated representation memory;
- major-axis direction error;
- active primitive or raster-cell count;
- seed diversity over 16 deterministic seeds;
- qualitative merge potential.

Direction error is measured from the major axis of a common evaluation raster against the requested local impact direction.

## E1 protocol

The **RUN / EXPORT E1 SUITE** action evaluates:

- 24 deterministic impact events;
- four impact-speed levels;
- three incident-angle levels;
- two volume levels;
- varying deterministic seeds and directions;
- all five representation methods.

The suite writes:

```text
results/phase1/E1_representation_metrics.csv
results/phase1/E1_representation_summary.csv
```

The summary reports mean generation cost, mean memory estimate, mean direction error, mean primitive count, and the number of unique binary coverage signatures.

## Interpretation rule

No candidate is promoted solely because it is fastest or uses the least memory.

Phase 2 should carry forward whichever representation or hybrid combination provides enough variation and surface-state continuity to justify dynamic accumulation, wetness, aging, and freezing.

## Known limitations

- this phase evaluates static marks only;
- field channels are represented structurally but are not yet updated over time;
- no dripping, spreading, puddling, transfer, or drying is simulated;
- the premade baseline uses generated fixed templates rather than external PNG art;
- local raster resolution is intentionally modest for real-time comparison;
- visual realism still requires human review in addition to numeric metrics.
