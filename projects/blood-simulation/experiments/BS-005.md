# BS-005 - Gravity-Driven Surface Flow, Puddles, and Detached Droplets

**Phase:** 3  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `blood-simulation-v0.4.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-19

## Question

Can the persistent Phase 2 surface field support a lightweight gravity-driven flow model that produces wall streaking, floor pooling, and detached world-space droplets without becoming a full fluid simulation?

## Surface flow

For every wet, unfrozen blood cell, world gravity is projected into the supporting surface tangent plane:

```text
g_surface = g - dot(g, n) n
```

The projected vector is converted into the surface's local U/V basis.

Blood above an adhesion threshold transfers toward neighboring cells according to:

- local gravity direction;
- wetness;
- wall-flow rate;
- available amount above adhesion;
- simulation timestep.

This naturally makes blood move downward on vertical surfaces while leaving horizontal surfaces without a preferred gravity direction.

## Puddles

Horizontal surfaces use a separate pressure-spread approximation.

Cells containing more blood than nearby cells transfer a limited amount laterally.

This is not Navier-Stokes flow. It is a local equalization rule intended to produce wider, shallower floor deposits from accumulated wet blood.

## Supported wall bases

If downward wall flow reaches a shared edge whose neighboring surface faces upward, blood is transferred directly into that supporting surface.

The primary controlled case is:

```text
wall
  ↓
shared bottom edge
  ↓
floor
```

This is intentionally narrower than the future general connected-surface flow model.

## Unsupported edges

If wet blood reaches an edge without a supporting upward-facing neighbor, enough accumulated material can detach.

The detached material becomes a world-space droplet with:

- XYZ position;
- XYZ velocity;
- volume;
- radius;
- deterministic seed.

The source surface loses the detached amount.

## World-space droplets

Detached droplets:

1. accelerate under world gravity;
2. move through the true-XYZ room;
3. cast a segment against the planar surface set;
4. deposit a new procedural stain on the first surface they hit.

The landing event derives a new local impact direction, speed, incident angle, volume, and seed from the world-space droplet state.

## E3 export

The Phase 3 lab exports:

```text
results/phase3/E3_gravity_flow.csv
```

The deterministic 45-second sweep records:

- wet cells;
- frozen cells;
- active world droplets;
- puddle cells;
- total blood amount;
- moved amount;
- cumulative detached droplets;
- cumulative landed droplets.

## Success condition

The approximation is useful if it can:

- turn a wall deposit into downward streaks;
- accumulate blood at supported wall bases;
- spread accumulated floor blood laterally;
- detach droplets at unsupported edges;
- redeposit detached blood into the persistent field;
- remain stable under a fixed 120 Hz substep.

## Scope boundary

General arbitrary corner transfer remains a later phase.

Phase 3 only performs direct same-surface flow, supported downward wall-to-floor transfer, and unsupported-edge detachment.
