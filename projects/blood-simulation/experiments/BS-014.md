# BS-014 - Single-Reservoir Drip Runner

**Phase:** 6  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `blood-simulation-v0.7.1`  
**Date:** 2026-10-20

## Question

Can a dense wall stain produce one localized drip that travels toward the floor while the parent stain remains substantially stationary?

## Motivation

The v0.7.0 ribbon pass improved visual thickness but could make too much attached blood appear mobile at once. The desired behavior is closer to a single gravity run: a dense region nominates one outlet, a bounded packet forms there, that packet descends, leaves a narrow residue, and finishes as one hanging or falling drop.

## Reference behavior

The comparison simulator uses sparse seeded drip objects with their own reservoir and downward progress rather than advancing an entire stain. Phase 6.1 adopts that behavioral idea while preserving the NM-PM surface-field representation and deterministic source selection.

## Method

Phase 5 dense connected-region selection is retained, but Phase 6.1 limits each dense component to one outlet and each surface to two simultaneous candidate outlets.

A successful outlet gathers a bounded reservoir from a five-by-five neighborhood centered on the dense source. Each contributing cell retains at least 72 percent of its local amount or the body threshold, whichever is greater. The runner is rejected and its collected material is returned if the available reservoir is too small.

Each runner stores:

- surface and current field cell;
- root field cell;
- deterministic seed;
- reservoir;
- wetness and age;
- cells-per-second travel rate;
- a trail budget derived from remaining distance to the gravity-facing edge.

The runner advances along projected surface gravity. It deposits a small center-weighted trail and retains a final reserve for the terminal drop. A small deterministic lateral deviation is allowed only at sparse intervals.

When the runner reaches the gravity-facing edge, the remaining reservoir becomes the existing hanging-tip representation, which then releases through the existing world-space droplet solver.

A circular six-cell source neighborhood is permanently marked for the current simulation session so the same dense region does not repeatedly seed new drips.

## Success criteria

BS-014 is successful if:

- one dense source creates one runner;
- capturing the runner conserves field amount plus runner reservoir;
- the same seeded region cannot immediately create a second runner;
- the runner advances toward lower world Z;
- trail deposition conserves field amount plus runner reservoir before detachment;
- the runner reaches the surface edge and produces a hanging tip;
- earlier Phase 5 outlet, pooling, landing, and contact tests remain valid.
