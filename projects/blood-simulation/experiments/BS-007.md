# BS-007 - Sparse Drip Channels and Stable Puddle Formation

**Phase:** 4  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `blood-simulation-v0.5.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-19

## Problem

The Phase 3 wall-flow approximation allowed nearly every wet cell to participate in gravity-driven transfer.

That caused two visual failures:

- large portions of an impact stain appeared to move together;
- walls could turn into dense vertical curtains instead of producing a few localized drips.

Phase 3 floor spreading also behaved too much like repeated directional transfer and could produce unstable edge cases around supporting floors.

## Sparse drip-source model

Phase 4 separates the stationary stain from active drip channels.

Only a small subset of wet wall cells may become a drip source.

A candidate must satisfy:

- non-horizontal supporting surface;
- sufficient blood amount;
- sufficient wetness;
- local peak across the gravity-flow direction;
- deterministic spatial selection;
- minimum spacing from another active source;
- per-surface source limit;
- site cooldown after a source expires.

The source-density control changes how likely eligible peaks are to become active channels.

## Channel motion

Each active source advances one local field cell at a time along projected gravity.

Only a limited amount is moved on each step.

The current cell retains residual blood.

This means the source gradually creates a thin trail while the majority of the original splat remains stationary.

## Drip frequency

Drip channels have independent deterministic timing.

Detached droplets also share a global cooldown.

A source that reaches an edge is retired for a site cooldown before the same region may generate another source.

This prevents a single region from producing a continuous stream of particles.

## Support probing

Exact surface adjacency is still used when available.

Phase 4 also adds a short downward support probe at an edge.

This solves partial-contact geometry such as a box side ending directly above the room floor even when the box edge is not an exact full-edge topology match.

If an upward-facing surface exists immediately below the edge:

```text
drip reaches edge
      ↓
support probe
      ↓
floor detected
      ↓
transfer to floor field
```

No world droplet is spawned in that case.

## Unsupported detachment

A world-space droplet is created only when:

- an active drip channel reaches an unsupported edge;
- the moved packet contains enough blood;
- the global detachment cooldown is clear;
- the active droplet cap has not been reached.

## Puddle solver

Upward-facing horizontal surfaces use an 8-neighbor isotropic pressure-spread approximation.

Cardinal and diagonal neighbors participate.

Outflow is bounded by:

- available blood above adhesion;
- local amount differences;
- wetness;
- puddle spread rate;
- timestep.

Using eight directions instead of four reduces diamond-shaped spreading and produces approximately circular puddle footprints.

## Collision correction

World-droplet swept collision now accepts very near surface intersections.

The earlier Phase 3 minimum-hit distance could reject a floor hit when a droplet began almost directly on the floor.

Combined with support probing, this prevents the observed below-floor particle stream.

## Success criteria

BS-007 is successful if:

- most of a wall splat remains stationary;
- only a few narrow drips form;
- drip sites are spatially separated;
- edge detachment is intermittent rather than continuous;
- box-side blood transfers onto a floor directly underneath;
- puddles expand in rounded footprints;
- world droplets do not continue below a valid floor.
