# BS-003 - Procedural Blood Morphology and Static Edge Continuation

**Phase:** 2  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `blood-simulation-v0.3.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-19

## Question

Can a procedurally generated blood deposit produce more recognizable impact morphology than the Phase 1 blob-like prototypes while remaining deterministic and surface-local?

## Visual target

The Phase 2 morphology aims for a physically suggestive stain rather than a decorative splash sprite.

The generator combines:

- a dense central deposit;
- irregular harmonic perimeter distortion;
- perimeter lobes;
- direction-biased streaks;
- satellite droplets;
- fine mist at higher impact energies;
- elongation derived from impact direction, speed, incident angle, and volume.

It intentionally does not reproduce glossy mid-air splash sprites. The target is a persistent stain that can plausibly remain on a surface.

## Deterministic construction

A fixed seed controls all irregularity.

The same:

```text
seed
direction
speed
incident angle
volume
```

produces the same morphology.

Changing only the seed changes the small-scale perimeter, streak, lobe, and satellite arrangement without changing the high-level physical controls.

## Edge problem

Phase 1 could draw generated marks beyond the finite bounds of the surface on which they were created. This produced visible floating or hanging geometry.

Phase 2 replaces that behavior with explicit surface topology.

Every planar surface exposes four finite edges. Shared world-space edge segments are detected and linked.

When a static deposit sample crosses a linked edge:

```text
source local point
        ↓
shared world-space edge
        ↓
neighbor surface
        ↓
neighbor local coordinates
```

The distance beyond the source edge becomes distance inward from the matching edge on the neighbor.

This allows examples such as:

- crate top → crate side;
- floor → back wall;
- side wall → back wall.

If no matching neighbor exists, the sample is clipped.

## Scope boundary

This is **static deposit continuation**, not fluid flow.

No gravity-driven liquid transfer across edges is simulated in BS-003. Dynamic transfer remains a later experiment and can reuse the same topology.

## Success condition

The generated stain must:

- remain attached to finite surfaces;
- avoid floating geometry at open edges;
- continue across real shared edges;
- preserve deterministic output;
- retain recognizable directionality and satellite structure.

## Known limitations

- the model remains an approximation;
- surface roughness and absorption are not yet material parameters;
- edge continuation currently assumes exact shared planar edges;
- partial floor/object contact edges are not treated as full adjacency;
- no liquid redistribution occurs after deposition.
