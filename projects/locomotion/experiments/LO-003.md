# LO-003 - Static Standing Pose Solver

**Phase:** 2  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `locomotion-v0.3.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-20

## Question

Can the locomotion study solve anatomically constrained static two-leg standing poses from explicit pelvis and foot targets without stretching limbs, flipping knees, or using stability metrics as hidden correction forces?

## Scope

LO-003 is the first phase that changes the body pose.

It still does not implement walking.

The solver receives:

- requested pelvis position;
- requested left ankle target;
- requested right ankle target;
- a preferred knee-bend direction.

It returns:

- a solved body pose;
- a shared feasible pelvis-height interval;
- exact or clamped ankle solutions;
- left and right knee positions;
- reach margins;
- approximate hip, knee, and ankle angles;
- comfortable/outer joint-envelope status.

## Analytic leg solve

Each leg is treated as a two-link chain:

```text
hip ---- upper leg ---- knee ---- lower leg ---- ankle
```

For upper-leg length `a`, lower-leg length `b`, and hip-to-ankle distance `d`, the knee is found from the triangle defined by those three lengths.

The along-axis distance from hip to the knee-plane center is:

```text
x = (a^2 - b^2 + d^2) / (2d)
```

The perpendicular knee offset is:

```text
h = sqrt(a^2 - x^2)
```

The preferred bend direction is projected onto the plane perpendicular to the hip-to-ankle axis. Phase 2 uses body-forward (+Y) as the normal knee preference.

This provides a deterministic branch choice instead of allowing the knee to flip arbitrarily between equally valid triangle solutions.

## Pelvis-height feasibility

Before solving either leg, LO-003 computes the vertical pelvis interval compatible with both foot targets.

The upper bound is controlled by maximum geometric extension:

```text
d_max = upper + lower
```

The lower bound uses the knee outer flexion guardrail from LO-001 to avoid solving a pose that would require excessive knee folding.

For each leg, horizontal hip-to-ankle separation is removed from the available reach using the Pythagorean relation. The left and right intervals are intersected to form one shared pelvis-height window.

If the requested pelvis height lies outside that window, only pelvis Z is clamped.

The HIGH TEST preset exists specifically to verify this behavior.

## Unreachable targets

An explicit foot target may be completely unreachable even after pelvis-height adjustment.

LO-003 does not stretch a leg to meet it.

Instead:

1. the target is marked unreachable;
2. the requested target remains visible in the harness;
3. the solved ankle is clamped to the nearest distance permitted by the two-link chain;
4. segment lengths remain unchanged.

The OVERREACH TEST preset deliberately exercises this failure path.

This is preferable to silently violating anatomy.

## Foot policy

Feet remain flat in LO-003.

The solved ankle translates the LO-001 heel, forefoot, and toe markers without rotating the foot.

This is sufficient for static standing and unequal-height tests, but not for slopes, heel strike, forefoot rocker, or toe-off. Those remain later research problems.

## Joint-angle diagnostics

The standing solver estimates:

- hip flexion/extension;
- hip abduction/adduction;
- knee flexion;
- ankle plantarflexion/dorsiflexion.

These values are checked against the comfortable and outer envelopes established by LO-001.

The angle model is intentionally simplified and tied to the current flat-foot standing problem. It is not a complete clinical joint-coordinate implementation.

## Presets

The native harness contains bounded test cases rather than a large wall of controls.

### NEUTRAL

Reconstruct the LO-001 neutral stance.

### LOW

Lower the pelvis while keeping both feet flat and require both knees to flex.

The first candidate used a pelvis height of 0.82 m. Windows CI showed that the resulting pose required about 36.9 degrees of ankle dorsiflexion, beyond the LO-001 outer 16-degree guardrail. The retained flat-foot LOW preset was therefore changed to 0.97 m.

This is a useful boundary rather than a tuning failure: a substantially deeper crouch should eventually permit heel rise / forefoot support instead of forcing the ankle through an impossible flat-foot angle.

### WIDE

Move both feet laterally and verify symmetric solving.

### SPLIT

Place one foot forward and the other backward.

### LEFT STEP

Raise the left support surface and solve unequal foot heights.

### HIGH TEST

Request a pelvis height above shared reach and verify automatic pelvis-height clamping.

### OVERREACH TEST

Request ankle positions beyond possible leg reach and verify target rejection without limb stretching.

## Interaction with LO-002

The solved Phase 2 body is passed through the existing LO-002 mass and support analysis.

This is the first direct composition of two locomotion phases:

```text
LO-001 anatomical contract
          |
          v
LO-003 standing pose solve
          |
          v
LO-002 COM + support measurement
```

The dependency direction is conceptual rather than chronological. LO-003 reuses LO-001 geometry and LO-002 measurement code, but support metrics do not alter the solved pose.

## Automated checks

LO-003 tests verify:

1. neutral shared pelvis window is feasible;
2. neutral pelvis is not clamped;
3. neutral knee position is preserved;
4. neutral legs satisfy outer limits;
5. low stance bends both knees;
6. low knees select the forward branch;
7. low stance remains within outer limits;
8. wide stance remains mirror symmetric;
9. wide stance remains geometrically reachable;
10. split stance preserves exact ankle targets;
11. raised-foot heel lands on the requested elevated surface;
12. raised-foot pose remains reachable;
13. too-high pelvis clamps downward;
14. extreme overreach reports no shared feasible pelvis;
15. overreach fallback preserves original leg segment lengths.

The LO-001 and LO-002 test suites remain active in the same CTest run.

## Rejected Phase 2 assumption

The initial deep flat-foot crouch assumed that knee flexion alone could absorb a large pelvis drop. The analytic solver showed that this transfers excessive rotation into the ankle.

The rejected assumption is:

```text
deeper crouch
    ->
same flat foot
    ->
just bend the knee more
```

The later foot-contact model should instead be allowed to progress toward heel rise and forefoot support when a deeper crouch requires it.

## Success condition

LO-003 succeeds if later stepping experiments can request static pelvis and foot targets while receiving:

- deterministic knees;
- fixed segment lengths;
- explicit reach failure;
- useful joint-envelope diagnostics;
- a stable body pose consumable by the LO-002 measurement layer.

## Intentional omissions

LO-003 does not implement:

- moving foot trajectories;
- gait phase;
- foot planting state transitions;
- automatic support/contact detection;
- pelvis motion driven by momentum;
- active balance forces;
- slopes or foot rotation;
- running;
- ragdoll behavior.

The next experiment should introduce exactly one procedural step rather than immediately attempting continuous walking.
