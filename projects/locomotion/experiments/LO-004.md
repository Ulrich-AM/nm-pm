# LO-004 - One Procedural Support-to-Support Step

**Phase:** 3  
**Status:** Implemented candidate  
**Milestone:** `locomotion-v0.4.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-21

## Question

Can the locomotion study generate one complete step from explicit support and landing conditions while preserving a planted foot, maintaining swing-foot clearance, respecting LO-001 anatomical limits, and terminating in another valid LO-003 standing pose?

## Why one step first

LO-004 deliberately does not alternate steps continuously.

Continuous walking can hide errors because a flawed end state immediately becomes the next start state. Phase 3 instead treats one step as a bounded transaction:

```text
double support
      |
      v
preload
      |
      v
single support + swing
      |
      v
touchdown
      |
      v
double support
      |
      v
valid static end pose
```

If this transaction is trustworthy, LO-005 can investigate repetition and alternating sides.

## Step plan

The default plan is intentionally fixed:

- step distance: 0.28 m;
- peak swing clearance: 0.05 m;
- nominal duration: 0.82 s;
- liftoff phase: 0.10;
- touchdown phase: 0.90;
- pelvis reach reserve: 0.004 m.

Either leg can be selected as the swing leg.

The opposite leg becomes the planted support leg.

## Stage model

LO-004 exposes four explicit stages.

### PRELOAD

Both feet remain in support. The swing ankle is still at its original position while the pelvis begins progressing toward the final stance.

### SWING

Only the planted foot contributes to the support polygon. The swing ankle follows the generated trajectory.

### LANDING

The swing foot has reached its landing target and double support is restored while the pelvis finishes the step.

### COMPLETE

The phase equals 1.0 and the resulting body is a valid static target pose suitable for the next experiment.

The stage model is intentionally explicit because later gait logic should react to contact events rather than deriving support only from a periodic animation clock.

## Swing trajectory

Horizontal motion uses a quintic smoother-step interpolation:

```text
s(u) = 6u^5 - 15u^4 + 10u^3
```

This gives zero first derivative at the start and end of horizontal travel.

Vertical swing clearance uses:

```text
z_lift(u) = h sin^2(pi u)
```

where `h` is the requested peak clearance.

This also begins and ends with zero vertical velocity while providing one clear midpoint maximum.

The current foot remains flat during swing. Foot pitch and heel/toe rocker behavior remain intentionally deferred.

## Toe-clearance metric

The toe marker from LO-001 is measured directly against the flat ground plane.

Because any physical step begins and ends at zero clearance, the useful Phase 3 metric is the minimum clearance across the central 20 to 80 percent of swing rather than the mathematical minimum over the complete trajectory.

The default plan requires more than 12 mm of central swing clearance.

LO-004 also records peak toe clearance and compares it against the requested 50 mm trajectory height.

## Planted-foot invariant

The support ankle target remains exactly equal to its starting ankle position throughout the transaction.

At every sampled phase:

```text
support_slip = |solved_support_ankle - starting_support_ankle|
```

The default plan is considered valid only when maximum support slip remains below numerical tolerance.

This is the first locomotion experiment to make planted-foot slip a formal metric.

## Pelvis policy

The pelvis advances toward the midpoint of the completed stance.

Pelvis height is selected from LO-003 reach geometry only.

The nominal reference height is retained whenever possible. If current leg geometry requires a lower pelvis, the target is placed just inside the shared feasible height window using a small reach reserve.

LO-002 COM or support margin never modifies the pelvis target.

This keeps the earlier architecture rule intact:

```text
support / COM measurement
          !=
hidden pose correction
```

## Support analysis

The solved body is passed through LO-002 at every sample.

During PRELOAD, LANDING, and COMPLETE:

```text
support = DOUBLE
```

During SWING:

```text
support = opposite planted foot only
```

LO-004 records support margin but does not attempt to make it positive.

A statically negative single-support margin is therefore an observation, not a solver failure.

## Future flight compatibility

LO-004 is still foot-specific, but it does not redefine the global support model to require a foot at all times.

The existing LO-002 NONE support state remains valid and untouched.

That is intentional preparation for later jumping experiments where:

```text
DOUBLE / SINGLE FOOT SUPPORT
             |
             v
takeoff
             |
             v
NONE
             |
             v
flight
             |
             v
landing
```

Likewise, Phase 3 does not introduce a generic hand/contact planner prematurely. Ledge climbing and parkour can later generalize the same contact concepts without forcing LO-004 to solve those problems now.

## Native harness

The Phase 3 harness provides:

- right-swing and left-swing variants;
- one-shot PLAY / PAUSE / RESET controls;
- 0.5x and 1.0x playback;
- a draggable normalized phase control;
- front, side, and three-quarter views;
- complete swing trajectory overlay;
- start and landing markers;
- support polygon;
- whole-body COM;
- live step stage;
- swing phase;
- toe clearance;
- support slip;
- reach and joint-limit status.

The plan geometry itself is fixed in the UI. LO-004 is an experiment, not yet a locomotion tuning tool.

## Automated checks

LO-004 adds eighteen checks:

1. duration is positive;
2. phase zero begins in PRELOAD;
3. PRELOAD uses double support;
4. mid-step uses one planted foot;
5. LANDING restores double support;
6. phase one reaches COMPLETE;
7. swing starts at the original ankle;
8. swing ends at the landing target;
9. planted support foot does not slide;
10. central swing toe clearance exceeds 12 mm;
11. peak toe clearance matches the requested plan;
12. all sampled poses remain geometrically reachable;
13. all sampled poses remain inside outer joint limits;
14. pelvis targets avoid hidden LO-003 height clamping;
15. final pelvis advances forward;
16. final COM remains finite;
17. left/right one-step plans mirror laterally;
18. the default step plan validates as a complete transaction.

LO-001, LO-002, and LO-003 tests remain active in the same CTest run.

## Success condition

LO-004 succeeds when one step can begin from a valid static pose and terminate in another valid static pose while preserving:

- planted-foot position;
- explicit support-state transitions;
- positive swing clearance;
- anatomical reach;
- outer joint limits;
- deterministic left/right behavior;
- compatibility with LO-002 measurement.

## Intentional omissions

LO-004 does not implement:

- alternating repeated steps;
- cadence adaptation;
- speed-driven stride length;
- automatic footstep planning;
- dynamic balance correction;
- automatic contact detection;
- swing-foot pitch or heel/toe rocker;
- terrain height queries;
- physical impulse generation;
- jumping or flight;
- upper-body counter-swing;
- ragdoll behavior.

The next experiment should determine whether the completed state can become the next step's initial state without phase discontinuity or accumulated planted-foot error.
