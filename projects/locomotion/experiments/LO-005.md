# LO-005 - Alternating Event-Sequenced Steps

**Phase:** 4  
**Status:** Implemented candidate  
**Milestone:** `locomotion-v0.5.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-21

## Question

Can the validated LO-004 step transaction repeat across alternating left/right feet without accumulated support-foot drift, pose discontinuity, knee-branch reversal, hidden pelvis correction, or phase error?

## Scope

LO-005 is the first repeated gait experiment.

It does **not** yet vary walking speed.

The controller keeps these quantities fixed:

- step length: 0.28 m between successive footfalls;
- swing clearance: 0.045 m;
- step duration: 0.82 s;
- liftoff phase: 0.10;
- touchdown phase: 0.90.

The default sequence contains six completed steps.

## State handoff

LO-004 always began from the original neutral stance. That is insufficient for repeated gait.

LO-005 instead carries a world-space stance state:

```text
left ankle
right ankle
pelvis
```

At the end of a step, that completed stance becomes the exact starting stance of the next step.

The next swing side alternates deterministically:

```text
RIGHT -> LEFT -> RIGHT -> LEFT ...
```

No joint pose is copied as an authored animation frame. Each sample is still solved from current targets through LO-003.

## Step length versus swing travel

The 0.28 m value describes distance between successive footfalls, not the distance traveled by a foot during every swing.

The sequence begins from an aligned neutral stance, so the first foot moves 0.28 m.

After that initialization step, the trailing foot passes the planted foot and lands 0.28 m ahead of it. Its world-space swing travel is therefore approximately 0.56 m.

This distinction is important for later speed-dependent stride work.

## Pelvis progression

For each step, the desired end pelvis lies over the midpoint of the two completed ankle targets.

The first LO-005 candidate reused LO-004's whole-step pelvis smoother. That assumption failed on the second step: at normalized phase 0.1917, the trailing swing-side ankle reached 16.1883 degrees dorsiflexion, slightly beyond the LO-001 16-degree outer guardrail.

The failure occurred before significant swing-foot lift. The issue was forward timing: the pelvis advanced faster than the trailing foot early in the repeated stride.

The retained Phase 4 policy therefore keeps forward pelvis progression at its starting value through PRELOAD, then advances it using the same normalized swing-transfer interpolation between liftoff and touchdown.

This keeps pelvis progression synchronized with the foot actually transferring forward instead of solving the limit violation by weakening the anatomical guardrail or shrinking clearance.

Pelvis height is still recomputed only from LO-003 reach geometry. A small reach reserve keeps the target away from exact singular extension.

LO-002 COM and support margins remain observers and do not move the pelvis.

## Rejected repeated-gait timing assumption

The rejected assumption was:

```text
one-step pelvis timing
       +
repeat the step
       =
valid repeated gait
```

LO-004 began from aligned feet, while later alternating steps begin with one foot trailing. Reusing the same whole-step pelvis timing therefore changed the trailing leg geometry enough to cross the ankle guardrail.

The corrected Phase 4 timing couples forward pelvis transfer to swing transfer only. This is a useful distinction between a valid isolated step and a valid repeated gait.

## Handoff continuity

For every adjacent pair of steps, LO-005 compares:

```text
pose(step_i, phase=1)
pose(step_i+1, phase=0)
```

across every anatomical joint.

Two formal metrics are retained:

```text
max_handoff_joint_delta
max_handoff_pelvis_delta
```

A valid sequence requires both to remain below numerical tolerance.

This is stronger than checking only ankle positions because a knee or upper-body discontinuity could otherwise be hidden at the boundary.

## Planted-foot accumulation

Each step still uses the LO-004 planted-foot invariant.

LO-005 evaluates the maximum support ankle error across all samples and all steps.

Because every completed stance becomes later input, even a tiny systematic error could accumulate. The sequence therefore rejects repeated support slip rather than assuming each step is independent.

## Knee-branch continuity

The analytic leg solution has two possible geometric knee branches.

LO-003 already chooses the body-forward branch. LO-005 verifies this choice across the complete repeated sequence by projecting each solved knee away from its hip-to-ankle axis and checking that the bend remains oriented toward body-forward (+Y).

A valid handoff cannot silently flip a knee to the opposite analytic branch.

## Event-sequenced controller

The playback controller stores:

```text
current step index
elapsed time inside current step
completed handoff count
finished state
```

When elapsed step time reaches the fixed step duration, the controller emits the logical handoff by:

1. advancing the completed-step count;
2. alternating the swing side;
3. resetting local step time;
4. using the completed stance as the next initial stance.

The controller stops after the requested integer number of steps.

A normalized sequence phase exists for visualization and deterministic scrubbing, but step identity is preserved explicitly rather than treating the entire walk as one repeating sine phase.

## Support sequence

Every individual step preserves the LO-004 stage model:

```text
PRELOAD  -> DOUBLE
SWING    -> LEFT or RIGHT
LANDING  -> DOUBLE
COMPLETE -> DOUBLE
```

The support foot alternates at each completed handoff.

The global NONE state remains untouched for future flight experiments.

## Native harness

The Phase 4 harness adds:

- repeated PLAY / PAUSE / RESET;
- deterministic 4-, 6-, and 8-step sequences;
- event-driven handoff counter;
- left/right swing display;
- normalized sequence scrubber;
- 0.5x and 1.0x playback;
- camera tracking of the moving pelvis;
- all planned footfall markers;
- current swing trajectory;
- support polygon and COM;
- support-slip metric;
- handoff pose-delta metric;
- knee-branch state;
- final forward distance.

Stride length and cadence remain fixed.

## Automated checks

LO-005 adds twenty checks:

1. the default sequence contains multiple steps;
2. the first swing is right;
3. the second swing is left;
4. all swing sides alternate;
5. first handoff pose is continuous;
6. first handoff pelvis is continuous;
7. the second step begins from the first landing;
8. the second mid-step uses the correct support foot;
9. all repeated poses remain reachable;
10. all repeated poses remain inside outer joint limits;
11. no sample requires hidden pelvis clamping;
12. knee branches remain forward;
13. planted support feet do not accumulate slip;
14. all handoff joint deltas remain below tolerance;
15. all handoff pelvis deltas remain below tolerance;
16. every swing retains central toe clearance;
17. the sequence advances the pelvis across several steps;
18. the controller performs a one-step handoff when the step completes;
19. the controller stops after the requested integer number of steps;
20. the complete default sequence validates.

LO-001 through LO-004 tests remain active in the same CTest run.

## Success condition

LO-005 succeeds if a fixed gait can repeat for several steps while preserving:

- exact step-to-step pose continuity;
- alternating support;
- planted-foot invariance;
- anatomical reach;
- outer joint limits;
- forward knee branch;
- useful swing clearance;
- deterministic stopping.

## Intentional omissions

LO-005 does not implement:

- desired walking velocity;
- cadence adaptation;
- stride adaptation;
- acceleration or deceleration;
- turning;
- backward or lateral gait;
- dynamic balance correction;
- automatic terrain contacts;
- foot pitch / heel-to-toe rocker;
- arm counter-swing;
- running;
- flight or jumping.

LO-006 should add desired speed as an independent input and determine whether cadence, step length, stance/swing timing, and body excursion can vary continuously without breaking the handoff invariants established here.
