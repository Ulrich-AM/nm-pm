# LO-006 - Velocity-Driven Whole-Body Walking

**Phase:** 5  
**Status:** Implemented  
**Milestone:** `locomotion-v0.6.1`  
**Date:** 2026-10-21

## Question

Can the fixed repeated gait from LO-005 accept a desired forward walking speed and adapt step spacing, cadence, support timing, pelvis coordination, trunk counter-rotation, and arm swing without losing planted-foot accuracy or violating the existing anatomical solver?

## Scope

LO-006 is the first phase that treats walking speed as an input.

It does not yet implement acceleration between arbitrary velocities. A command is selected, the gait parameters are derived from that command, and the bounded sequence is then executed at those fixed parameters.

The validated Phase 5 walking envelope is intentionally conservative:

```text
0.40 m/s <= desired walking speed <= 0.80 m/s
```

Commands outside this interval clamp to the nearest validated endpoint.

This envelope is a property of the current flat-foot research model, not a proposed biological boundary between walking and running.

## Evidence boundary

Healthy-human research consistently shows that walking speed changes spatiotemporal and joint-level gait variables rather than changing cadence alone. Faster walking commonly combines altered step/stride length and cadence, while support timing also changes.

LO-006 uses that qualitative evidence to justify a speed-dependent controller, but the exact mapping below is an NM-PM heuristic rather than a fitted human regression.

References used for this boundary include:

- Fukuchi et al. 2018, public healthy walking dataset: https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.4640
- Fukuchi et al. 2019, systematic review of walking-speed effects: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31248456/
- Bohannon & Andrews 2020, outdoor walking speed and cadence meta-analysis: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33030707/

## v0.6.1 reference-motion refinement

The first published LO-006 milestone, `locomotion-v0.6.0`, proved the steady speed envelope and introduced pelvis/trunk/arm coordination, but visual inspection exposed a structural upper-body problem.

The Phase 0 neutral wrists sit about 1.52 shoulder-widths from the sagittal body center. Rotating that already-wide pose as a rigid arm chain made stronger arm swing look increasingly unnatural.

Four supplied in-place FBX references were therefore analyzed:

- walk;
- slow run;
- medium run;
- fast run.

All four use the same Mixamo skeleton and compatible bind transforms. The original FBX files are not stored in NM-PM. Only derived measurements are retained under `reference/`.

The files are not treated as animation targets or as measured physical speeds. Their labels are qualitative, and their timing is not monotonic with the words `slow`, `medium`, and `fast`. The measurements are used only to identify broad intersegmental relationships and plausible envelopes.

The supplied walk clip showed:

- wrist lateral distance averaging about 0.78 shoulder-widths from the torso center;
- wrist lateral range approximately 0.67-0.83 shoulder-widths;
- elbow flexion across the two arms approximately 15-58 degrees;
- very strong contralateral leg/hand timing;
- pelvis and thorax yaw approximately opposing one another.

The run clips show substantially greater elbow flexion in many frames and weaker direct leg-to-hand correlation. Those run measurements are retained for later walk/run transition work, not copied into the present walking controller.

## Velocity profile

A normalized speed coordinate is computed across the validated interval.

The current controller maps that coordinate into:

- successive footfall spacing;
- step duration;
- cadence;
- swing clearance policy;
- liftoff phase;
- touchdown phase;
- double-support share;
- whole-body coordination amplitudes.

The default endpoints are:

| Quantity | 0.40 m/s | 0.80 m/s |
|---|---:|---:|
| successive footfall spacing | 0.26 m | 0.28 m |
| swing clearance | 0.038 m | 0.038 m |
| double-support edge per step | 0.12 | 0.05 |
| pelvis yaw amplitude | 0.6 deg | 1.2 deg |
| pelvis obliquity amplitude | 0.7 deg | 1.5 deg |
| lateral pelvis shift | 0.009 m | 0.017 m |
| thorax yaw amplitude | 2.4 deg | 4.0 deg |
| arm forward corridor amplitude | 0.075 m | 0.115 m |
| wrist lateral target | 0.78 shoulder widths | 0.72 shoulder widths |
| elbow flexion base | 28 deg | 34 deg |
| elbow flexion added at arm-drive extreme | 6 deg | 10 deg |

Swing clearance is intentionally held at the LO-005 validated 0.038 m value. The first LO-006 candidate scaled clearance down with speed, but Windows CI showed that the cruise profile then fell below the preserved 12 mm central toe-clearance invariant. The invariant was kept and the speed-scaling assumption was rejected rather than weakening the validation threshold.

A second candidate kept the clearance invariant but used a broader 0.40-1.00 m/s command range with 0.26-0.40 m successive-footfall spacing. At 0.70 m/s, step 2 phase 0.13 reached 16.009 degrees of left-ankle dorsiflexion against the existing 16-degree outer guardrail. The violation was only 0.009 degrees, but the guardrail was retained. Phase 5 therefore narrows its validated flat-foot envelope to 0.40-0.80 m/s and 0.26-0.28 m footfall spacing. A subsequent timing-only adjustment reduced the remaining cruise violation to 16.0388 degrees but did not remove it. A later Windows envelope test with a 0.30 m fast-end spacing still reached 16.1128 degrees dorsiflexion on step 2. A 0.285 m retry reduced that peak to 16.0042 degrees, which was still too close to the guardrail to retain. The final fast-end spacing is 0.28 m, leaving actual numerical margin rather than relying on tolerance.

Step duration closes the requested steady-state speed directly:

```text
step_duration = step_length / desired_speed
cadence_spm = 60 / step_duration
```

This means the steady-state pelvis displacement of one successive-footfall interval per step matches the commanded speed by construction.

## Pelvis coordination

LO-005 translated the pelvis through the gait but treated the hip frame as orientation-neutral.

LO-006 adds three bounded pelvis effects.

### Horizontal yaw

The relative forward separation of the feet defines a signed leg signal:

```text
leg_signal =
    clamp((right_ankle_y - left_ankle_y) / step_length, -1, +1)
```

Pelvis yaw follows this signal with a speed-dependent amplitude.

The result rotates the left/right hip offsets before the legs are re-solved. The feet therefore remain exact targets instead of being dragged by a post-process visual rotation.

Pelvic rotation is included because transverse pelvis motion is a measured component of human walking and its relationship to step length changes with speed. Liang et al. found the total contribution to step length to be small in normal walking, which is one reason LO-006 keeps the amplitude restrained rather than exaggerating it.

Reference:

- Liang et al. 2014: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2013.06.006
- Whitcome et al. 2017: https://doi.org/10.1002/ar.23551

### Lateral shift

During single support the pelvis moves slightly toward the planted foot.

The shift uses a smooth single-support weight:

```text
support_weight = sin^2(pi * swing_phase)
```

so the lateral offset is zero at liftoff and touchdown and reaches its maximum near mid-swing.

LO-002 support metrics still do not drive this value. The shift is an explicit gait policy, not a hidden COM correction.

### Pelvic obliquity

The swing-side hip is allowed a small vertical offset through rotation of the hip pair around the forward axis.

The amplitude is intentionally small and returns to zero at the support transitions.

## Custom hip solve

Pelvis yaw and roll alter hip positions.

LO-006 therefore does not rotate an already-solved leg after the fact.

Instead:

1. the desired pelvis point is established;
2. the reference left/right hip offsets are rotated;
3. each resulting hip point is passed into the existing LO-003 analytic leg solver through a per-leg proxy pelvis;
4. the original ankle targets remain unchanged;
5. the solved knees and hips are written back into the body.

This preserves upper- and lower-leg segment lengths while making pelvis orientation part of the actual constraint solve.

## Thorax counter-rotation

The v0.6.1 refinement no longer defines the shoulder frame as a small residual of pelvis yaw.

The supplied walk reference shows pelvis and thorax yaw approximately opposing one another. LO-006 therefore gives them separate signed targets. The retained pelvis amplitude is intentionally smaller than the reference because the current flat-foot leg model reaches the ankle guardrail:

```text
pelvis_yaw = -pelvis_amplitude * leg_signal
thorax_yaw = +thorax_amplitude * leg_signal
```

The diagnostic value `thorax_yaw - pelvis_yaw` exposes the relative torso twist directly.

This remains a compact coordination policy rather than a spinal biomechanics model.

The v0.6.1 pelvis-yaw sweep also exposed a useful limitation. Larger reference-direction yaw repeatedly pushed the trailing ankle just above the unchanged 16-degree dorsiflexion guardrail, even while foot targets remained exact. The retained 0.6-1.2 degree range therefore stays conservative until foot pitch / rocker mechanics can absorb more realistic pelvis rotation.

## Reference-informed upper-limb solve

The v0.6.0 arm rule rotated the complete neutral arm chain around the shoulder. That preserved limb lengths but also preserved the excessively wide neutral wrist position.

v0.6.1 replaces that rule with a two-link upper-limb solve.

The controller first creates a torso-relative wrist target from:

- thorax orientation;
- shoulder width;
- a normalized lateral hand corridor;
- contralateral forward arm drive;
- a speed-dependent elbow-flexion target.

For each arm:

```text
shoulder
    |
    v
torso-relative wrist corridor
    |
    +-- desired lateral distance
    +-- desired fore/aft displacement
    +-- elbow-flexion-derived chain distance
    |
    v
analytic shoulder-elbow-wrist solve
```

The upper-arm and forearm lengths are taken from the unchanged LO-001 reference body.

The target elbow flexion determines shoulder-to-wrist distance:

```text
d^2 = upper^2 + fore^2 + 2*upper*fore*cos(elbow_flexion)
```

A mild torso-relative elbow pole then chooses the bend plane.

The hand tip continues along the solved forearm direction while preserving its reference segment length.

### Arm state

Contralateral leg separation still provides the arm-drive target:

```text
right leg ahead -> left arm drive forward
left leg ahead  -> right arm drive forward
```

but the runtime arm state stores a dimensionless drive and drive velocity rather than an absolute shoulder angle.

A damped second-order response moves that persistent state toward the target.

This is important for later acceleration and walk/run transition work because the arm state can change target without being reset or replaced by an animation clip.

### Reference envelope

The current walking controller validates:

- wrist lateral corridor 0.60-0.90 shoulder-widths;
- actual configured walking targets 0.72-0.78 shoulder-widths;
- elbow flexion inside the supplied walk range;
- exact upper-arm and forearm lengths.

The run references are intentionally **not** used to force a running elbow pose during LO-006.

## Comparison modes

The harness exposes four whole-body modes:

```text
LEGS ONLY
+ PELVIS
+ PELVIS + TRUNK
FULL BODY
```

These modes use the same desired speed and footstep plan.

They exist so visual review can isolate which layer changes the gait instead of allowing added upper-body motion to hide a lower-body defect.

## Speed-dependent support timing

The retained speed map reduces double-support share as walking speed rises.

At the slow endpoint:

```text
liftoff = 0.12
touchdown = 0.88
double-support fraction = 0.24
```

At the fast endpoint:

```text
liftoff = 0.05
touchdown = 0.95
double-support fraction = 0.10
```

The transition remains explicit and deterministic. Collision-based contact detection is still deferred.

## Preserved invariants

Whole-body coordination is not allowed to move either foot target.

At every sampled coordinated pose:

```text
left_solved_ankle  == left_LO005_target
right_solved_ankle == right_LO005_target
```

The evaluation also retains:

- LO-005 planted-foot slip checks;
- complete-step handoff checks;
- LO-003 reach checks;
- LO-001 outer joint guardrails;
- central toe-clearance checks.

COM is recomputed after pelvis, trunk, and arm coordination so LO-002 observes the actual displayed body.

## Automated checks

The v0.6.1 refinement expands the Phase 5 suite to thirty-two headline checks.

In addition to the original speed, foot, reach, and support invariants, the suite now requires:

- independent thorax-yaw scaling;
- speed-dependent arm forward corridor;
- pelvis and thorax yaw to oppose at meaningful gait phases;
- exact upper-arm and forearm length preservation;
- wrist positions inside a torso-relative corridor;
- elbow flexion inside the supplied walking reference envelope;
- a procedural wrist corridor substantially narrower than the Phase 0 neutral pose;
- overlap between the cruise wrist corridor and the supplied walk reference;
- stateful arm response toward a changed target;
- complete sampled body-envelope validity across the walking-speed range.

All LO-001 through LO-005 suites remain active.

## Intentional omissions

LO-006 still does not implement:

- changing speed while already walking;
- start from rest;
- stopping;
- acceleration or deceleration;
- predictive balance correction;
- XCoM-driven foothold adjustment;
- foot pitch / heel rocker / toe-off;
- terrain adaptation;
- running;
- flight;
- jumping;
- hand contacts or climbing;
- head stabilization;
- detailed spinal motion;
- direct FBX playback or runtime dependence on Mixamo data.

Those omissions are important because LO-007 should test transitions between velocity states without changing the steady-state gait question solved here.
