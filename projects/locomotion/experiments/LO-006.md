# LO-006 - Velocity-Driven Whole-Body Walking

**Phase:** 5  
**Status:** Implemented candidate  
**Milestone:** `locomotion-v0.6.0`  
**Date:** 2026-10-21

## Question

Can the fixed repeated gait from LO-005 accept a desired forward walking speed and adapt step spacing, cadence, support timing, pelvis coordination, trunk counter-rotation, and arm swing without losing planted-foot accuracy or violating the existing anatomical solver?

## Scope

LO-006 is the first phase that treats walking speed as an input.

It does not yet implement acceleration between arbitrary velocities. A command is selected, the gait parameters are derived from that command, and the bounded sequence is then executed at those fixed parameters.

The validated Phase 5 walking envelope is intentionally conservative:

```text
0.40 m/s <= desired walking speed <= 1.00 m/s
```

Commands outside this interval clamp to the nearest validated endpoint.

This envelope is a property of the current flat-foot research model, not a proposed biological boundary between walking and running.

## Evidence boundary

Healthy-human research consistently shows that walking speed changes spatiotemporal and joint-level gait variables rather than changing cadence alone. Faster walking commonly combines altered step/stride length and cadence, while support timing also changes.

LO-006 uses that qualitative evidence to justify a speed-dependent controller, but the exact mapping below is an NM-PM heuristic rather than a fitted human regression.

References used for this boundary include:

- Fukuchi et al. 2018, public healthy walking dataset: https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.4640
- Fukuchi et al. 2019, systematic review of walking-speed effects: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31248456/
- Bohannon & Andrews 2020, outdoor walking speed and cadence meta-analysis: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33030707/

## Velocity profile

A normalized speed coordinate is computed across the validated interval.

The current controller maps that coordinate into:

- successive footfall spacing;
- step duration;
- cadence;
- swing clearance;
- liftoff phase;
- touchdown phase;
- double-support share;
- whole-body coordination amplitudes.

The default endpoints are:

| Quantity | 0.40 m/s | 1.00 m/s |
|---|---:|---:|
| successive footfall spacing | 0.26 m | 0.40 m |
| swing clearance | 0.030 m | 0.038 m |
| double-support edge per step | 0.16 | 0.08 |
| pelvis yaw amplitude | 1.5 deg | 4.0 deg |
| pelvis obliquity amplitude | 0.6 deg | 1.5 deg |
| lateral pelvis shift | 0.008 m | 0.018 m |
| trunk counter-yaw amplitude | 0.8 deg | 2.8 deg |
| arm-swing target amplitude | 8 deg | 24 deg |

Step duration closes the requested steady-state speed directly:

```text
step_duration = step_length / desired_speed
cadence_spm = 60 / step_duration
```

This means the steady-state pelvis displacement of one successive-footfall interval per step matches the commanded speed by construction.

## Pelvis coordination

LO-005 translated the pelvis through the gait but treated the hip frame as orientation-neutral.

LO-006 adds three bounded pelvis effects.

### Horizontal yaw

The relative forward separation of the feet defines a signed leg signal:

```text
leg_signal =
    clamp((right_ankle_y - left_ankle_y) / step_length, -1, +1)
```

Pelvis yaw follows this signal with a speed-dependent amplitude.

The result rotates the left/right hip offsets before the legs are re-solved. The feet therefore remain exact targets instead of being dragged by a post-process visual rotation.

Pelvic rotation is included because transverse pelvis motion is a measured component of human walking and its relationship to step length changes with speed. Liang et al. found the total contribution to step length to be small in normal walking, which is one reason LO-006 keeps the amplitude restrained rather than exaggerating it.

Reference:

- Liang et al. 2014: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.gaitpost.2013.06.006
- Whitcome et al. 2017: https://doi.org/10.1002/ar.23551

### Lateral shift

During single support the pelvis moves slightly toward the planted foot.

The shift uses a smooth single-support weight:

```text
support_weight = sin^2(pi * swing_phase)
```

so the lateral offset is zero at liftoff and touchdown and reaches its maximum near mid-swing.

LO-002 support metrics still do not drive this value. The shift is an explicit gait policy, not a hidden COM correction.

### Pelvic obliquity

The swing-side hip is allowed a small vertical offset through rotation of the hip pair around the forward axis.

The amplitude is intentionally small and returns to zero at the support transitions.

## Custom hip solve

Pelvis yaw and roll alter hip positions.

LO-006 therefore does not rotate an already-solved leg after the fact.

Instead:

1. the desired pelvis point is established;
2. the reference left/right hip offsets are rotated;
3. each resulting hip point is passed into the existing LO-003 analytic leg solver through a per-leg proxy pelvis;
4. the original ankle targets remain unchanged;
5. the solved knees and hips are written back into the body.

This preserves upper- and lower-leg segment lengths while making pelvis orientation part of the actual constraint solve.

## Trunk counter-rotation

A separate trunk-yaw target opposes pelvis yaw.

The shoulders and arm roots rotate around the chest using:

```text
upper_yaw = pelvis_yaw + trunk_counter_yaw
```

The counter-rotation amplitude remains smaller than the pelvis/leg signal and scales with walking speed.

This is a coordination approximation, not a spinal biomechanics model.

## Arm swing

Normal walking commonly includes contralateral arm swing.

LO-006 uses the same leg-separation signal:

```text
right leg ahead -> left arm forward
left leg ahead  -> right arm forward
```

The direct target is speed-dependent, but the native playback harness does not snap the arms directly to this value.

Each arm maintains angular state and angular velocity in a damped second-order response:

```text
acceleration =
    stiffness * (target - angle)
    - damping * angular_velocity
```

The spring and damping coefficients scale modestly with walking speed.

This approximates the observation that human arm swing contains substantial passive-dynamic behavior while still being actively coordinated.

Reference:

- Collins et al. 2009, Dynamic arm swinging in human walking: https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2009.0664
- Umberger 2008, effects of suppressing arm swing: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbiomech.2008.05.024

The Phase 5 arms remain simple shoulder-flexion pendulums. Elbow choreography and hand behavior are not introduced.

## Comparison modes

The harness exposes four whole-body modes:

```text
LEGS ONLY
+ PELVIS
+ PELVIS + TRUNK
FULL BODY
```

These modes use the same desired speed and footstep plan.

They exist so visual review can isolate which layer changes the gait instead of allowing added upper-body motion to hide a lower-body defect.

## Speed-dependent support timing

The retained speed map reduces double-support share as walking speed rises.

At the slow endpoint:

```text
liftoff = 0.16
touchdown = 0.84
double-support fraction = 0.32
```

At the fast endpoint:

```text
liftoff = 0.08
touchdown = 0.92
double-support fraction = 0.16
```

The transition remains explicit and deterministic. Collision-based contact detection is still deferred.

## Preserved invariants

Whole-body coordination is not allowed to move either foot target.

At every sampled coordinated pose:

```text
left_solved_ankle  == left_LO005_target
right_solved_ankle == right_LO005_target
```

The evaluation also retains:

- LO-005 planted-foot slip checks;
- complete-step handoff checks;
- LO-003 reach checks;
- LO-001 outer joint guardrails;
- central toe-clearance checks.

COM is recomputed after pelvis, trunk, and arm coordination so LO-002 observes the actual displayed body.

## Intentional omissions

LO-006 still does not implement:

- changing speed while already walking;
- start from rest;
- stopping;
- acceleration or deceleration;
- predictive balance correction;
- XCoM-driven foothold adjustment;
- foot pitch / heel rocker / toe-off;
- terrain adaptation;
- running;
- flight;
- jumping;
- hand contacts or climbing;
- head stabilization;
- detailed spinal motion.

Those omissions are important because LO-007 should test transitions between velocity states without changing the steady-state gait question solved here.
