Anatomically Constrained Procedural Human Locomotion

An NM-PM study investigating how human-like walking, running, balance, support changes, and terrain adaptation can be generated from anatomy, body state, momentum, foothold choice, and environmental constraints rather than primarily replaying fixed gait clips.

Study03Current experimentLO-010 KLAW published-method reconstructionPhasePhase 9 implementedLast releaselocomotion-v0.9.0 (LO-009)StatusActive / KLAW baseline pending lead inspection before v0.10.0
LO-001 through LO-009 formed an independent bottom-up exploration. Visual evaluation remained march-like despite continuous state, articulated support, and stance dynamics. Literature review showed substantial overlap with KLAW (Bruderlin & Calvert, 1989). The study therefore pivots at LO-010: reconstruct the published KLAW methodology as an explicit external baseline, then modify it deliberately. NM-PM does not claim authorship of KLAW.

Measured gait and authored motion may be used as reference evidence, but the study will investigate which behaviors must be explicitly controlled and which can emerge from anatomy, contacts, gravity, momentum, and limited guidance.

Research Workflow

  1. Define one narrow question.
  2. Preserve the previous harness.
  3. Implement the smallest controlled experiment.
  4. Test pure math independently of the window.
  5. Use visual recordings when metrics cannot describe motion adequately.
  6. Preserve failures and superseded approaches.
  7. Promote shared architecture only after reuse is demonstrated.

This combines the preserved-phase approach of Blood Simulation with the shared-core rule used by Sprite Projection.

The basis is right-handed and renderer-independent. Raylib receives a drawing-time conversion only. Camera state never changes locomotion state. Physical calculations use meters, seconds, kilograms when mass is introduced, and radians internally.

LO-002 - Segment Mass Model

The Phase 1 reference uses fourteen body segments: head, trunk, paired upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, shanks, and feet. Segment mass fractions and longitudinal COM ratios use the arithmetic mean of the female and male adjusted Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov tables reported by de Leva (1996). This avoids selecting one sex as the default reference subject while preserving a measured human-like distribution.

The rounded published mass fractions total 0.99995, so the implementation divides by the observed sum instead of assuming it is exactly one.

The LO-001 mannequin has fewer anatomical landmarks than the original anthropometric model. Published longitudinal COM ratios are therefore mapped onto the nearest simplified segment lines. This mapping is documented as an approximation.

Center of Mass

C_segment = P + r(D - P)

C_body = sum(m_i C_i) / sum(m_i)

Each segment COM is placed along its proximal-to-distal line. Whole-body COM is the mass-weighted average of all fourteen segment COMs. In the neutral reference pose it lies close to the sagittal midline and approximately 1.02 m above the ground.

Support Geometry

Support is explicitly classified as DOUBLE, LEFT, RIGHT, or NONE. Each active foot contributes its LO-001 heel-to-forefoot rectangular patch. The convex hull of all active contact corners becomes the support polygon.

The signed support margin is the shortest distance from a measured ground point to the support-polygon boundary. It is positive inside, negative outside, and unavailable with no active support.

These values do not control the body. A negative support margin is reported, not corrected.

XCoM Diagnostic

omega0 = sqrt(g / h)
XCoM = x + v / omega0

LO-002 includes Hof's velocity-aware extrapolated COM as a diagnostic. Fixed velocity presets let the same static body demonstrate how momentum can move the predicted stability point outside the current support area even while the ordinary COM projection remains inside.

XCoM is not yet a stepping target or balance controller.

LO-003 - Static Standing Solver

Phase 2 receives a pelvis target, left and right ankle targets, and a preferred knee-bend direction. It returns a complete static body pose plus reach and joint-limit diagnostics. Upper-body joints translate with the pelvis while each leg is solved independently from fixed segment lengths.

The solver deliberately uses bounded presets rather than exposing dozens of tuning controls: neutral, low, wide, split stance, raised-and-forward left step, excessive pelvis height, and an impossible overreach case.

The first LOW candidate placed the pelvis at 0.82 m. The resulting flat-foot solve required about 36.9° ankle dorsiflexion and failed the 16° outer guardrail. The retained LOW preset uses 0.97 m. A deeper crouch should eventually permit heel rise / forefoot support rather than violating the ankle.

Shared Pelvis-Height Feasibility

Before solving the legs, each target defines a vertical pelvis interval compatible with the current horizontal hip-to-ankle separation. The upper bound comes from full leg extension. The lower bound is constrained by the LO-001 outer knee-flexion guardrail. The left and right intervals are intersected.

d_max = upper + lower

pelvis_max_z = ankle_z + sqrt(d_max^2 - horizontal^2)

If the requested pelvis height lies outside the shared interval, only pelvis Z is clamped. The HIGH TEST preset exists to make this correction visible rather than hiding it inside IK.

Analytic Two-Link Leg IK

x = (a^2 - b^2 + d^2) / (2d)
h = sqrt(a^2 - x^2)

The knee lies at distance x along the hip-to-ankle axis and h away from that axis. Body-forward (+Y) is projected onto the perpendicular knee plane to select one deterministic human-like bend branch. This prevents arbitrary knee flipping between mathematically valid solutions.

Approximate hip flexion/extension, hip abduction/adduction, knee flexion, and ankle plantar/dorsiflexion are checked against the comfortable and outer LO-001 envelopes.

Reach Failure Policy

An impossible target is not solved by stretching a limb. The requested marker remains visible, while the solved ankle clamps to the nearest legal chain length. The harness draws the difference between requested and solved positions. Segment lengths therefore remain invariant even in the deliberate OVERREACH TEST.

This distinction will matter during stepping. A future step planner must be able to reject or revise an unreachable foothold instead of receiving a visually plausible but anatomically invalid leg.

The default plan moves the swing ankle 0.28 m forward over 0.82 s, with liftoff at normalized phase 0.10 and touchdown at 0.90. The end pose is solved through LO-003 rather than copied from an authored gait frame.

Swing Trajectory

Horizontal travel uses quintic smoother-step interpolation so the swing target begins and ends with zero horizontal velocity:

s(u) = 6u^5 - 15u^4 + 10u^3

Vertical lift uses a separate zero-end-velocity profile:

z_lift(u) = h sin^2(pi u)

The retained default peak lift is 0.045 m. Feet remain flat during LO-004, so foot pitch and heel/toe rocker remain visible limitations rather than being hidden inside the trajectory function.

The first candidate used 0.050 m clearance. Windows CI found a peak swing-ankle dorsiflexion of about 16.47°, slightly beyond the LO-001 16° outer guardrail. The limit was not widened. Phase 3 retains 0.045 m and records the result as evidence that future swing-foot pitch may be necessary for larger clearance.

Support Stages

PRELOAD begins in double support. During SWING only the opposite planted foot contributes to the LO-002 support polygon. LANDING and COMPLETE restore double support.

The pelvis advances toward the midpoint of the completed stance. Its height can move inward from the LO-003 reach boundary, but COM and support margin never alter the pelvis target. A negative support margin remains a measurement.

Toe Clearance and Planted-Foot Slip

LO-004 promotes two motion quantities into explicit validation metrics.

Planted-foot slip is the distance between the solved support ankle and its starting position. The default plan is invalid if this exceeds numerical tolerance.

Toe clearance is measured directly from the LO-001 toe-tip marker to the flat ground plane. Since every valid step naturally begins and ends at zero clearance, the phase reports the minimum clearance over the central 20 to 80 percent of swing as well as the peak value.

The default plan requires more than 12 mm central swing clearance and approximately 45 mm peak clearance while remaining inside LO-001 outer joint limits.

The fixed 0.28 m value describes spacing between successive footfalls. After the initial aligned step, the trailing foot travels about 0.56 m in world space to pass the planted foot and land 0.28 m ahead.

Handoff Continuity

Every adjacent step compares the complete solved pose at the previous COMPLETE state with the next PRELOAD state. The study records maximum joint-position delta and pelvis-position delta over all boundaries.

max_handoff_joint_delta
max_handoff_pelvis_delta

Planted support ankle error is accumulated across the full sequence rather than checked only per isolated step. Knee bend direction is also verified so the analytic IK branch cannot silently flip at a handoff.

Repeated-Gait Pelvis Timing

The first LO-005 candidate reused LO-004's whole-step pelvis interpolation. Windows CI found the first outer-limit violation on step 2 at phase 0.1917: the trailing swing-side ankle reached 16.1883° dorsiflexion against the 16° LO-001 guardrail.

The swing foot had barely lifted at that point. The problem was forward timing: the pelvis advanced faster than the trailing foot early in the stride.

The retained Phase 4 policy keeps forward pelvis progression fixed through PRELOAD and then advances it using the same normalized swing-transfer interpolation between liftoff and touchdown.

That correction reduced the first violation to 16.0414°, but the flat trailing foot still crossed the guardrail. A dense numerical sweep showed that the remaining problem was horizontal transfer timing rather than clearance alone.

v = u + u(1-u)
horizontal = smootherstep(v)

The retained transfer front-loads horizontal foot progression through this monotonic phase warp while the outer quintic still gives zero endpoint velocity. LO-005 uses 0.038 m repeated-gait clearance with the warped transfer; LO-004 keeps its valid 0.045 m isolated-step value.

The anatomical limit was not widened. The result shows that a valid isolated-step trajectory does not automatically generalize to a repeated split-stance gait.

Sequence Controller

The controller stores current step index, elapsed local step time, completed handoff count, and finished state. When a step completes, the controller advances the handoff, alternates swing side, and carries the completed stance forward. It stops exactly after the requested integer number of steps.

A normalized sequence phase is available for deterministic inspection and scrubbing, but step identity is preserved explicitly rather than reduced to one endlessly wrapping animation phase.

LO-005 is repeated motion, not speed-controlled walking. Step length, clearance, and duration remain fixed so LO-006 can introduce desired speed without mixing research questions.

LO-006 - Velocity Profile

Phase 5 accepts desired forward walking speed as a continuous steady-state command. The currently validated range is 0.40 to 0.80 m/s. Values outside that interval clamp to the nearest tested endpoint; the range is a limitation of the present flat-foot model, not a biological walk/run boundary.

desired speed
    |
    +-- successive footfall spacing
    +-- step duration
    +-- cadence
    +-- liftoff / touchdown timing
    +-- coordination amplitudes

Step duration is defined by step_length / desired_speed, so the steady-state pelvis advance of one successive-footfall interval per step closes the commanded speed exactly. Step length increases across the envelope while double-support share decreases.

The exact mapping is an NM-PM controller heuristic. Published gait research supports the qualitative fact that walking speed changes spatiotemporal and joint-level gait variables, but LO-006 does not claim that its endpoint values are a fitted human regression.

Pelvis and Thorax Coordination

LO-006 keeps small pelvic obliquity and a lateral shift toward the current support side. Those terms fade to zero at the support transitions. Pelvis yaw and thorax yaw are now separate targets with opposing signs rather than one upper-body residual rotation.

pelvis_yaw = -pelvis_amplitude * leg_signal
thorax_yaw = +thorax_amplitude * leg_signal

relative_torso_twist =
    thorax_yaw - pelvis_yaw

Pelvis rotation is part of the actual hip geometry. The reference hip offsets are rotated first and each leg is solved again against the unchanged LO-005 ankle targets, so whole-body motion cannot create foot skate.

The retained pelvis motion is deliberately smaller than the supplied walk reference because the flat-foot leg model reaches the existing 16° dorsiflexion guardrail. A component decomposition showed that lateral shift was essentially harmless and yaw was modest, while pelvic obliquity alone could cross the guardrail. v0.6.1 therefore retains 1.0-1.5° pelvis yaw and the lateral shift, but limits obliquity to 0.3-0.5°. Larger reference-sized roll should be revisited when heel/toe rocker and foot pitch are available.

d^2 = upper^2 + fore^2

Rejected LO-006 Assumptions

The first LO-006 candidate reduced swing clearance at slower commanded speeds. Windows CI showed that this caused the cruise profile to fall below the preserved LO-005 minimum central toe-clearance invariant.

The invariant was not weakened. The retained Phase 5 controller keeps the validated 0.038 m repeated-gait flat-foot lift across the speed range while step spacing, cadence, and support timing remain speed-dependent. A second candidate retained that clearance but used a broader 0.40-1.00 m/s / 0.26-0.40 m spacing map. At 0.70 m/s, step 2 phase 0.13 reached 16.009° left-ankle dorsiflexion against the existing 16° outer guardrail. The retained flat-foot envelope is therefore 0.40-0.80 m/s with 0.26-0.28 m successive-footfall spacing. Earlier-liftoff timing reduced one intermediate violation to 16.0388°. A later 0.30 m fast-end candidate still reached 16.1128° dorsiflexion on step 2, and a 0.285 m retry still reached 16.0042°. The retained fast-end spacing is therefore 0.28 m, leaving numerical margin instead of widening anatomy.

This is another consequence of the current flat-foot swing model. More sophisticated foot pitch and rocker behavior should be investigated later rather than using speed as a reason to relax an existing clearance test.

Post-LO-006 Flow Audit

LO-006 v0.6.1 passed its numerical validation and corrected the most obvious upper-body geometry problem from v0.6.0, but visual inspection still showed a highly regular, march-like gait. This is now treated as a research result rather than a request for more cosmetic tuning.

The Phase 3-5 lineage uses the step transaction as the fundamental unit of motion:

valid stance
    |
    v
planned swing
    |
    v
touchdown
    |
    v
COMPLETE
    |
    v

This architecture is useful for proving reachability, planted-foot accuracy, support transitions, and deterministic continuity. It is weaker at producing the sense that the body already has momentum and is continuously moving through changing contacts.

Observed structural causes

  • Step completion is too important. A left/right transaction boundary remains perceptually obvious even when the pose itself is continuous.
  • The pelvis is primarily commanded. Root position is sampled from gait policy rather than carried through support changes as persistent position/velocity state.
  • The stance foot is effectively flat. Heel strike, weight acceptance, heel rise, forefoot support, and toe-off are not yet part of the mechanics.
  • Swing is one generic transfer. The current trajectory does not distinguish initiation, clearance, terminal extension, and contact preparation as separate functional jobs.
  • Liftoff and touchdown are prescribed by the clock. Contact cannot yet correct the timing model.
  • Whole-body coordination remains strongly driven by one leg-separation signal. v0.6.1 improved arm geometry but not the fundamental timing hierarchy.
  • Validation emphasizes legality, not flow. Segment lengths, joint limits, support slip, toe clearance, and handoff continuity can all pass while velocity/acceleration progression still looks mechanical.

The audit is preserved in experiments/LO-006-FLOW-AUDIT.md.

Reference interpretation

Bruderlin and Calvert's KLAW work is the strongest architecture reference for this correction: desired locomotion is decomposed into state/phase timing and locomotion-specific dynamics rather than relying only on joint interpolation. Chung and Hahn place stance-foot constraints and pelvis/root trajectory near the center of a hierarchical controller. Boulic, Magnenat-Thalmann, and Thalmann provide an empirical parameterized level-walk model useful as a comparison benchmark. Fu's 2025 thesis is useful mainly for the idea of persistent coupled limb phases rather than one global gait clock.

The study is not adopting any of these systems wholesale. The shared lesson is narrower: a natural walk is more likely to emerge when continuous body state and contact mechanics generate the motion, while gait rhythm organizes event readiness rather than directly animating every joint.

Revised Runtime Event Flow

LO-007 now implements the persistent-state foundation of this event flow. Later phases still need articulated stance, physical handoff/impact, functional swing, and dynamic body transport. The target relationship remains continuous, and several events are expected to overlap.

1. MOVEMENT INTENT
   desired direction / velocity

2. CONTINUOUS BODY TRANSPORT
   root position + velocity + angular state already exist

3. SUPPORT EVALUATION
   heel / flat / forefoot / airborne contacts

4. WEIGHT ACCEPTANCE
   current support begins carrying the body

5. STANCE ROLLOVER
   heel -> flat -> forefoot while the body passes over support

6. SWING READINESS
   free-limb phase/contact state approaches release

7. TOE-OFF / RELEASE
   departing foot stops contributing meaningful support

8. SWING INITIATION
   knee flexion / shortening creates clearance

9. MID-SWING TRANSPORT
   foot advances while root motion continues

10. TERMINAL SWING
    knee extends; foot prepares orientation for contact

11. CONTACT PREDICTION
    desired foothold is checked against body state and reach

12. HEEL STRIKE / TOUCHDOWN
    actual contact event occurs

13. STATE CORRECTION + WEIGHT TRANSFER
    contact updates continuing body/phase state

14. CONTINUE
    no global gait reset

This is an event relationship, not a proposed fourteen-state animation clip. A stance foot may still be in forefoot support while the opposite foot enters contact preparation; limb phases and root motion continue through the overlap.

The pure simulation advances at 120 Hz. The harness accumulates real frame time but locomotion state changes only on fixed ticks. Root forward position is integrated from persistent root velocity rather than sampled from a finite step phase. Contact timing

Release and touchdown are still predicted from limb phase readiness on flat ground. They are not yet physical collision events. Their new role is architectural: a contact-transition event can add bounded timing correction and enter the contact-history ring buffer without reconstructing the locomotion controller. A deterministic disturbance probe offsets one limb phase and verifies that timing recovers without a global reset.

The accepted candidate reports zero root-velocity and root-acceleration jumps in the steady kinematic transport model, planted-foot slip at numerical precision, segment-length error at numerical precision, and exact deterministic replay for identical fixed-tick runs. Phase 6 contains twenty-nine self-tests, and Windows CI passes all seven preserved CTest suites from LO-001 through LO-007.

Root / pelvis boundary

The persistent root and the displayed pelvis are not yet identical. Root Y advances continuously from root velocity, but the flat-foot LO-003 solve still keeps pose-solver pelvis Y near the midpoint of the current ankle targets. This preserves the unchanged ankle dorsiflexion guardrail instead of widening anatomy to make the new architecture fit.

This limitation is now explicit. LO-007 proves that persistent locomotion state can survive support changes, but it does not yet prove dynamic body passage over the planted stance foot. LO-008 will promote heel, forefoot, and toe contact into actual support mechanics; LO-009 will then compare locomotion-specific stance/root transport models.

The Phase 6 gait may still look march-like. That is expected at this checkpoint because foot rocker, dynamic stance, physical impact handoff, functional swing, pelvis/root flow, and reactive whole-body dynamics remain later experiments.

The rocker is not applied after solving the body. The active pivot and foot pitch determine the ankle target before the leg solver runs, so the articulated foot changes the kinematic constraint itself.

Support geometry

The LO-001 heel, toe-base, toe-tip, and foot-width measurements define the support geometry. Heel support uses a rear-foot region, flat support uses the full patch, forefoot support moves the patch forward and narrows it, and toe support narrows further toward the toe region. Airborne feet contribute no support patch. The current active regions are combined into the LO-002 support polygon.

Foot pitch is rate-limited to avoid abrupt rocker transitions. If the articulated request becomes anatomically invalid, the controller reduces root-follow and then reduces pitch toward flat rather than widening the LO-001 ankle, knee, or hip guardrails.

Flat versus articulated comparison

The Phase 7 harness can restart the same steady walking condition in LO-007 FLAT or LO-008 ARTICULATED mode. It displays active foot modes, pitch, ankle dorsiflexion, support polygon, root Y, displayed pelvis Y, and their forward mismatch. The comparison is intended to test whether articulated support can reduce the flat-foot transport restriction without sacrificing reachability or outer-limit validity.

Main-branch Windows CI builds all Phase 0 through Phase 7 executables and passes eight out of eight CTest suites. Phase 7 contains twenty-nine self-tests. A redundant support-polygon test typo was corrected before the v0.8.0 release trigger; runtime behavior was unchanged.

Current boundary

LO-008 is not yet a dynamic stance controller. Touchdown remains phase-predicted, the swing leg still uses temporary scaffolding, impact/weight transfer are deferred to LO-010, and the visible pelvis still uses conservative kinematic root-follow rather than a stance-dynamics model. LO-009 will compare the smallest useful dynamic stance/root-transport alternatives after visual QA of this checkpoint.

Biomechanics Basis for Later Phases

Gait timing is state-dependent

Walking contains stance, swing, and double-support structure, but speed changes cadence, stride, joint motion, and timing. Gait phase should coordinate events rather than directly scripting every joint.

Dynamic balance is not static COM centering

LO-002 now computes ordinary COM support and Hof's extrapolated COM side by side. Later foot-placement work can test whether velocity-aware state improves stepping without turning the metric into an invisible pelvis force.

Measured gait is comparison evidence

The Fukuchi public dataset provides healthy overground and treadmill kinematics and kinetics at different speeds. Later phases can compare generated pelvis, hip, knee, ankle, and temporal curves against measured human behavior without replaying the dataset as animation.

Segment mass is now explicit

The de Leva-based Phase 1 mass distribution gives later experiments a consistent body COM rather than averaging joints equally.

LO-009 Dynamic Stance (historical)

LO-009 / Phase 8 compared three support-driven root models on top of LO-008: kinematic LO-008 baseline (A), rigid virtual-leg (B), and compliant telescoping stance (C). It is released as locomotion-v0.9.0 and remains a preserved harness. Despite corrected pivot velocity, rigid length constraints, and root authority, visual evaluation still read as march-like alternating steps.

LO-010 KLAW Published-Method Reconstruction

LO-010 / Phase 9 reconstructs the published KLAW (Keyframe-Less Animation of Walking) methodology of Bruderlin and Calvert (SIGGRAPH 1989) as an explicit external procedural baseline. The 1989 dynamic model is the primary implementation source. The 1993 paper may clarify parameters and gait determinants but must not replace the 1989 dynamics with the later fully kinematic rewrite.

Hierarchy reconstructed in order: locomotion parameters → step symmetry / state-phase timings → walking state machine → stance/swing specialized dynamics → virtual-leg kinematics → gait determinants → upper body → pose. Control remains intentionally step-oriented (one step = double + single support).

Traceability for every major subsystem lives in reference/KLAW_RECONSTRUCTION.md. Ambiguous OCR equations are marked; missing thesis collision algebra is approximated and labeled LOW confidence.

KLAW Attribution

NM-PM does not claim authorship of KLAW. KLAW and its underlying methodology are credited to Armin Bruderlin and Thomas W. Calvert. LO-010 is an engineering reconstruction based on their published work. It is not claimed to be an exact original KLAW implementation.

Revised Roadmap

After LO-009, the study pivots from independently rediscovering KLAW-like mechanisms to reconstructing the published 1989 method first, then modifying it deliberately.

Current Limitations

  • Reference proportions are a controlled test subject, not a population model.
  • The Phase 1 mass table uses a male/female arithmetic mean rather than demographic-specific parameters.
  • Several frontal and transverse joint limits are provisional.
  • The knee is not yet modeled with coupled translation and axial rotation.
  • The foot support patch is planar and simplified.
  • LO-008 release/touchdown remains predicted from limb phase readiness rather than detected from physical collision.
  • The current analytic joint-angle diagnostics remain simplified.
  • Root velocity is persistent and LO-008 permits conservative root-follow, but the displayed pelvis is still kinematically placed rather than transported by a dynamic stance model.
  • Acceleration, deceleration, and stopping are intentionally deferred until continuous locomotion state and stance mechanics exist.
  • The 0.40-0.80 m/s envelope is a current-model validation range, not a human normative boundary.
  • Pelvis, thorax, wrist-corridor, and elbow targets are procedural controller heuristics constrained by broad reference envelopes rather than fitted subject-specific biomechanics.
  • Pelvis yaw remains deliberately conservative while the stance model is still kinematic.
  • LO-008 now articulates stance feet through heel/flat/forefoot/toe support, but touchdown impact, weight acceptance dynamics, and functional swing-foot control remain later work.
  • The current swing path remains one generic transfer curve rather than functional swing subphases.
  • LO-007 adds root velocity/acceleration and phase-continuity diagnostics, but jerk, stance angular acceleration, momentum, and impact continuity remain later work.
  • No imported rig is connected yet.
  • No physical actuation, terrain adaptation, jumping, or active balance controller exists yet.
