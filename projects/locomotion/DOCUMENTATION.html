Anatomically Constrained Procedural Human Locomotion

An NM-PM study investigating how human-like walking, running, balance, support changes, and terrain adaptation can be generated from anatomy, body state, momentum, foothold choice, and environmental constraints rather than primarily replaying fixed gait clips.

LO-004 is the first bounded locomotion transaction. One foot remains planted while the other follows a generated swing trajectory from liftoff to touchdown. It still does not repeat steps or apply active balance forces.

Measured gait and authored motion may be used as reference evidence, but the study will investigate which behaviors must be explicitly controlled and which can emerge from anatomy, contacts, gravity, momentum, and limited guidance.

Research Workflow

  1. Define one narrow question.
  2. Preserve the previous harness.
  3. Implement the smallest controlled experiment.
  4. Test pure math independently of the window.
  5. Use visual recordings when metrics cannot describe motion adequately.
  6. Preserve failures and superseded approaches.
  7. Promote shared architecture only after reuse is demonstrated.

This combines the preserved-phase approach of Blood Simulation with the shared-core rule used by Sprite Projection.

Coordinate Contract

+X = subject right
+Y = subject forward
+Z = up

X cross Y = Z

The basis is right-handed and renderer-independent. Raylib receives a drawing-time conversion only. Camera state never changes locomotion state. Physical calculations use meters, seconds, kilograms when mass is introduced, and radians internally.

Joint Envelopes

Maximum clinical ROM is not normal gait ROM. Enabled axes therefore have a comfortable envelope inside a wider outer research guardrail.

These are controller guardrails, not medical absolutes. Soucie et al. support several major sagittal ROM references and also show age and sex variation. Some Phase 0 frontal and transverse limits remain provisional. Joint coordinate descriptions will follow ISB recommendations where practical instead of inheriting renderer Euler angles.

LO-002 - Segment Mass Model

The Phase 1 reference uses fourteen body segments: head, trunk, paired upper arms, forearms, hands, thighs, shanks, and feet. Segment mass fractions and longitudinal COM ratios use the arithmetic mean of the female and male adjusted Zatsiorsky-Seluyanov tables reported by de Leva (1996). This avoids selecting one sex as the default reference subject while preserving a measured human-like distribution.

The rounded published mass fractions total 0.99995, so the implementation divides by the observed sum instead of assuming it is exactly one.

The LO-001 mannequin has fewer anatomical landmarks than the original anthropometric model. Published longitudinal COM ratios are therefore mapped onto the nearest simplified segment lines. This mapping is documented as an approximation.

Center of Mass

C_segment = P + r(D - P)

C_body = sum(m_i C_i) / sum(m_i)

Each segment COM is placed along its proximal-to-distal line. Whole-body COM is the mass-weighted average of all fourteen segment COMs. In the neutral reference pose it lies close to the sagittal midline and approximately 1.02 m above the ground.

Support Geometry

Support is explicitly classified as DOUBLE, LEFT, RIGHT, or NONE. Each active foot contributes its LO-001 heel-to-forefoot rectangular patch. The convex hull of all active contact corners becomes the support polygon.

The signed support margin is the shortest distance from a measured ground point to the support-polygon boundary. It is positive inside, negative outside, and unavailable with no active support.

These values do not control the body. A negative support margin is reported, not corrected.

XCoM Diagnostic

omega0 = sqrt(g / h)
XCoM = x + v / omega0

LO-002 includes Hof's velocity-aware extrapolated COM as a diagnostic. Fixed velocity presets let the same static body demonstrate how momentum can move the predicted stability point outside the current support area even while the ordinary COM projection remains inside.

XCoM is not yet a stepping target or balance controller.

LO-003 - Static Standing Solver

Phase 2 receives a pelvis target, left and right ankle targets, and a preferred knee-bend direction. It returns a complete static body pose plus reach and joint-limit diagnostics. Upper-body joints translate with the pelvis while each leg is solved independently from fixed segment lengths.

The solver deliberately uses bounded presets rather than exposing dozens of tuning controls: neutral, low, wide, split stance, raised-and-forward left step, excessive pelvis height, and an impossible overreach case.

The first LOW candidate placed the pelvis at 0.82 m. The resulting flat-foot solve required about 36.9° ankle dorsiflexion and failed the 16° outer guardrail. The retained LOW preset uses 0.97 m. A deeper crouch should eventually permit heel rise / forefoot support rather than violating the ankle.

Shared Pelvis-Height Feasibility

Before solving the legs, each target defines a vertical pelvis interval compatible with the current horizontal hip-to-ankle separation. The upper bound comes from full leg extension. The lower bound is constrained by the LO-001 outer knee-flexion guardrail. The left and right intervals are intersected.

d_max = upper + lower

pelvis_max_z = ankle_z + sqrt(d_max^2 - horizontal^2)

If the requested pelvis height lies outside the shared interval, only pelvis Z is clamped. The HIGH TEST preset exists to make this correction visible rather than hiding it inside IK.

Analytic Two-Link Leg IK

x = (a^2 - b^2 + d^2) / (2d)
h = sqrt(a^2 - x^2)

The knee lies at distance x along the hip-to-ankle axis and h away from that axis. Body-forward (+Y) is projected onto the perpendicular knee plane to select one deterministic human-like bend branch. This prevents arbitrary knee flipping between mathematically valid solutions.

Approximate hip flexion/extension, hip abduction/adduction, knee flexion, and ankle plantar/dorsiflexion are checked against the comfortable and outer LO-001 envelopes.

Reach Failure Policy

An impossible target is not solved by stretching a limb. The requested marker remains visible, while the solved ankle clamps to the nearest legal chain length. The harness draws the difference between requested and solved positions. Segment lengths therefore remain invariant even in the deliberate OVERREACH TEST.

This distinction will matter during stepping. A future step planner must be able to reject or revise an unreachable foothold instead of receiving a visually plausible but anatomically invalid leg.

LO-004 - One-Step Transaction

Phase 3 connects two valid standing states with one explicit sequence: PRELOAD, SWING, LANDING, and COMPLETE. Either leg can be chosen as the swing leg. The opposite ankle remains fixed at its initial target for the full transaction.

The default plan moves the swing ankle 0.28 m forward over 0.82 s, with liftoff at normalized phase 0.10 and touchdown at 0.90. The end pose is solved through LO-003 rather than copied from an authored gait frame.

Swing Trajectory

Horizontal travel uses quintic smoother-step interpolation so the swing target begins and ends with zero horizontal velocity:

s(u) = 6u^5 - 15u^4 + 10u^3

Vertical lift uses a separate zero-end-velocity profile:

z_lift(u) = h sin^2(pi u)

The default peak lift is 0.05 m. Feet remain flat during LO-004, so foot pitch and heel/toe rocker remain visible limitations rather than being hidden inside the trajectory function.

Support Stages

PRELOAD begins in double support. During SWING only the opposite planted foot contributes to the LO-002 support polygon. LANDING and COMPLETE restore double support.

The pelvis advances toward the midpoint of the completed stance. Its height can move inward from the LO-003 reach boundary, but COM and support margin never alter the pelvis target. A negative support margin remains a measurement.

Toe Clearance and Planted-Foot Slip

LO-004 promotes two motion quantities into explicit validation metrics.

Planted-foot slip is the distance between the solved support ankle and its starting position. The default plan is invalid if this exceeds numerical tolerance.

Toe clearance is measured directly from the LO-001 toe-tip marker to the flat ground plane. Since every valid step naturally begins and ends at zero clearance, the phase reports the minimum clearance over the central 20 to 80 percent of swing as well as the peak value.

The default plan requires more than 12 mm central swing clearance and approximately 50 mm peak clearance while remaining inside LO-001 outer joint limits.

Future Flight Boundary

LO-004 uses only DOUBLE and single-foot support, but it deliberately does not redefine locomotion to require a supporting foot. The LO-002 NONE state remains valid for future takeoff and flight experiments.

Native Harness

The Phase 3 harness preserves the NM-PM grayscale UI, Windows Arial path, sharp controls, large viewport, and bottom diagnostics strip. It provides right- or left-foot swing, PLAY / PAUSE / RESET, 0.5x and 1.0x playback, direct phase scrubbing, view presets, trajectory overlay, support polygon, COM, start/landing targets, toe clearance, support slip, reach status, and joint-limit status.

The default step geometry remains fixed in the UI. This phase evaluates the mechanism rather than becoming a general gait tuning application.

Validation

All earlier test suites remain active. LO-004 adds eighteen checks for stage ordering, support transitions, exact trajectory endpoints, planted-foot invariance, central toe clearance, peak clearance, geometric reach, outer joint limits, hidden pelvis-clamp avoidance, final pelvis progression, finite COM, left/right mirroring, and whole-plan validity.

Biomechanics Basis for Later Phases

Gait timing is state-dependent

Walking contains stance, swing, and double-support structure, but speed changes cadence, stride, joint motion, and timing. Gait phase should coordinate events rather than directly scripting every joint.

Dynamic balance is not static COM centering

LO-002 now computes ordinary COM support and Hof's extrapolated COM side by side. Later foot-placement work can test whether velocity-aware state improves stepping without turning the metric into an invisible pelvis force.

Measured gait is comparison evidence

The Fukuchi public dataset provides healthy overground and treadmill kinematics and kinetics at different speeds. Later phases can compare generated pelvis, hip, knee, ankle, and temporal curves against measured human behavior without replaying the dataset as animation.

Segment mass is now explicit

The de Leva-based Phase 1 mass distribution gives later experiments a consistent body COM rather than averaging joints equally.

Current Limitations

  • Reference proportions are a controlled test subject, not a population model.
  • The Phase 1 mass table uses a male/female arithmetic mean rather than demographic-specific parameters.
  • Several frontal and transverse joint limits are provisional.
  • The knee is not yet modeled with coupled translation and axial rotation.
  • The foot support patch is planar and simplified.
  • Support/contact stage is still prescribed by the one-step plan rather than detected from collision events.
  • Feet remain flat during swing and cannot pitch/roll with terrain.
  • The current analytic joint-angle diagnostics remain simplified.
  • Only one step is generated; there is no alternating repeated gait yet.
  • No imported rig is connected yet.
  • No speed-driven cadence, physical actuation, terrain adaptation, jumping, or active balance controller exists yet.
