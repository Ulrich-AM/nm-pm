Future Studies

Open-ended by design

NM-PM is not limited to the initial five studies. New projects may be added whenever a technical problem is interesting enough to investigate independently and document as a reusable system.

Possible directions

Procedural animation, compact simulation methods, visual correspondence, physically informed rendering, experimental interfaces, reusable test harnesses, or other systems that benefit from isolated experimentation.

Admission rule

A study should ask a concrete technical question, support measurable experiments, preserve failed approaches, and produce something understandable outside the project that originally inspired it.

Methodology

Research loop
QUESTIONDefine the technical problem.
PROTOTYPEBuild the smallest useful experiment.
OBSERVERecord behavior, evidence and failure.
REFINEChange the model from what was learned.
PRESERVEDocument and release useful milestones.
Experiments are not deleted simply because they fail. Failed approaches are part of the research record when they explain a limitation, reveal a hidden assumption, or motivate a better model.

Documentation

Living technical notebook

Each study maintains a standalone DOCUMENTATION.html. It is closer to a browsable technical notebook or small wiki than a formal academic paper.

Expected contents

Research questions, terminology, equations, implementation notes, experiment IDs, screenshots, extracted video frames, tables, failed approaches, limitations, references, current algorithms and version history.

Visual evidence

When behavior is difficult to explain in prose, screenshots or recordings should be requested and preserved. Useful video frames may be extracted into figures or contact sheets and referenced directly in the documentation.

Releases

Easy-to-run checkpoints

The repository preserves the journey. Releases preserve useful destinations. Not every experiment becomes a release.

AI Transparency

Development model

NM-PM is explicitly machine-assisted. Generative AI may contribute code, mathematical exploration, debugging strategies, prototype implementation, alternative approaches, documentation and organization.

Human-directed

Research goals, questions, concepts, evaluation, testing, reference selection, visual evidence, rejection of unsuitable approaches, design decisions and overall direction.

Machine-assisted

Implementation, derivation support, prototyping, debugging, alternative formulations, code generation, documentation support and research organization.

The purpose of disclosure is accuracy, not apology. Individual studies should describe machine assistance factually when it materially shaped the work.
